## 1. Overall verdict

Heroes of A Fallen Land is a solid assessment with good production art, well-written judgement scenarios and three varied minigames. It is held back by length, pacing, script polish and a few serious technical bugs.

At its core it currently plays like **a long questionnaire with a fantasy story in between**, not like a game. Around 40% of a candidate's time goes to reading unscored story, the story does not react to the candidate's choices (and sometimes contradicts them), and only the three minigames are actually played.

The scenarios themselves are the strongest part. Dilemmas like the starving pickpocket, the foreigner fee and the cursed villagers measure realistic workplace behaviour. The results report appears the moment the game ends, which is a genuine strength.

**In one line:** the assessment content is good, but the experience around it is too long, too passive and not polished enough. That matches the "long and boring" feedback from HR prospects.

**Overall: about 5 out of 10.** Strong assessment content, but the experience and length don't justify it yet.

### What an HR head would likely say in the debrief

> "The scenarios are good, and I like that it covers personality, judgement and cognitive ability in one go. But it took over an hour, and half of that was clicking through story. Our current test takes 25 minutes. I can't put 300 operators through this on their phones, and I can't justify an hour per employee for internal assessment either. The typos and the blank screen one of us got make me wonder how solid the scoring is. Interesting, but not now — come back when it's half the length and works on mobile."

**Likely decision: no, or at best "shortlist, revisit later".** Not because the assessment is bad, but because the trial makes every weakness visible while the strengths (the scenarios and the instant report) only appear at the very end.

### What would turn it into a yes

1. **About 30–40 minutes**, with a skip option for the story, keeping all ranking questions.
2. **Works reliably on a budget Android phone,** with no blank screens or broken controls.
3. **Clean script:** no typos, and the hero never contradicts the candidate's choices.
4. **A report that explains itself:** which game or question produced each score, plus a benchmark comparison.
5. **A 10-minute buyer demo** before HR is asked to sit through the full candidate version.

### Limits of this rating

The HR admin panel, the Bahasa Indonesia version and the phone experience were not reviewed, and this is one person's single run. Minigame 2's score of 0 was caused by the reviewer's tool connection, not the game, so it was not counted against reliability.

## 5. What works well

- **Art and atmosphere.** The backgrounds (festival town, forest, castle, cave, Keep of Magic, volcano, library) and character designs are strong and varied.
- **Hidden assessment.** Wrapping judgement questions in a quest makes it feel less like a test. Turning the rank-1 answer into the hero's spoken line is a good touch.
- **Ranking controls.** Drag-and-drop with Reset works reliably, and Next only appears once all four ranks are placed.
- **Scenario quality.** The items cover a useful spread: planning versus impulsiveness, collaboration, conflict handling, empathy, honesty, calm under pressure, adaptability and motivation. The oxygen-hallucination and "why do you seek this power?" questions are clever, indirect motivation probes.
- **Mix of measures.** Combining judgement questions with three cognitive minigames (memory, spatial reasoning, vocabulary) is a strong concept.
- **Minigame 3 (word puzzle)** is the most enjoyable: fast rounds, instant feedback, and difficulty that builds nicely from 3- to 10-letter words.
- **The final act has real payoffs:** the fake orb, the framed apprentice, Aurora's sacrifice and Bullaric's motive tie the story together.
- **Connection recovery.** After a "Connection Lost" message, "Try Again" restored the session at exactly the same point.
- **Instant results.** The report is available the moment the game ends.

## 6. Problems found

### 6.1 Technical bugs (highest priority)

1. **Same account open in two places breaks the story.** When the account was open in a second tab, that tab received empty story data. The dialogue box stayed blank, every click threw a JavaScript error (`SyntaxError: Unexpected end of JSON input`, then `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'split')`), and the candidate saw no error message. This is likely to happen in HR trials, where evaluators open links twice or share one demo account.
2. **Ranking stops working after a reconnect.** After "Connection Lost" → "Try Again", dragging rank numbers did nothing. It only started working again after the "?" help popup was opened and closed. A real candidate would be stuck.
3. **Minigame timer runs behind the tutorial.** In minigame 2 the timer showed 4:59 the moment the tutorial was closed, so time was already counting while instructions were on screen.
4. **Timed minigames keep running when the player is interrupted.** A real connection or device problem during a minigame would cost the candidate their score, as the reviewer's tool freeze did. Timers should pause or allow a retry.
5. **Two clicks per dialogue line.** A click while text is typing only finishes the line, so a second click is needed to advance. This adds hundreds of clicks.
6. **Objective screens only respond to a click on the small "Click here to continue" text,** unlike dialogue boxes, which respond anywhere.
7. **Slow first load (~15–20 seconds)** on a plain grey screen. Uncompressed WAV sound files of 0.6–1.9 MB each contribute.
8. **Engine version warning:** "Version of ink used to build story doesn't match current version of engine."
9. **Ranking tutorial timing:** on this account the ranking tutorial appeared at the third question, after two had already been answered.
10. **The end survey popup covers the results screen.**

### 6.2 User experience issues

- **Too long and too passive** (see section 4).
- **Choices rarely change anything.** After many choices the story carries on the same way, and several outcomes are skipped entirely (see Appendix C).
- **The hero sometimes acts against the candidate's answers,** for example "Test attacks the Demon Chief to let off the anger and frustration" and "We found it, so it's ours. We'll never give it to you!" after consistently calm, cooperative choices.
- **Minigame results don't affect the story.** The story carried on as a success after a score of 0.
- **"We recommend playing this game in a PC browser"** on the main menu discourages mobile-only candidates.
- **The login screen** has small, low-contrast "Forgot password?" and "Sign up" links, and the game is letterboxed with black bars.
- **No clear sense of remaining time.** Only a slowly moving percentage bar.

### 6.3 Script quality

- Around 15 typos and grammar errors (Appendix B).
- Around 20 story contradictions and dropped threads (Appendix C).
- Around 12 art and character mismatches (Appendix D).
- The narration switches between third person ("Test approaches…") and second person ("You and your party…").

## 7. Scoring and assessment concerns

1. **Characters praising answers teaches candidates the "right" ones.** For example, Durin says "You're selfless in seeking this power. I'll assist you." This makes faking easier on later questions and weakens a key advantage of game-based assessment. Reactions should stay neutral, or be equally positive for every option.
2. **The rank-1 answer is not reliably the hero's line.** In at least one case the rank-1 answer was spoken by another character (Hawke), and some rank-1 lines appear without a speaker portrait or name.
3. **The memory test is too easy.** With only three symbols, the reviewer scored 100%, and the correct answer is usually "No". The test likely can't tell strong candidates apart.
4. **The Visual Perception score looks inconsistent.** It showed 25 after 100% accuracy on both the memory test and the word puzzle. Check which game feeds which score, and make that visible in the report.
5. **The top trait may not match the answer pattern.** The reviewer consistently ranked cautious, plan-first, information-gathering and collaborative answers highest, but the top trait was "Innovative". One run proves nothing, but the mapping is worth a sanity check.
6. **The word puzzle tests English vocabulary in the English version.** For Indonesian candidates, that measures English skill rather than reasoning. Check that the Bahasa version uses Bahasa words.
7. **Culturally foreign content.** For example, "HALLOWEEN" (children in costume asking for candy) means little to most Indonesian candidates.

## 8. Fit for the Indonesian market

The likely audiences are HR in Indonesian organisations, including field organisations (mining, plantation, manufacturing, logistics, retail operations), and internal employee assessment.

- **Device:** most field candidates only have an Android phone, but the game recommends a PC.
- **Data:** large uncompressed audio files cost prepaid mobile data and slow loading on patchy connections.
- **Reading load:** about 300 lines of narration in small fonts is hard work for candidates who don't read much day-to-day, even in Bahasa Indonesia.
- **Link to the job:** a medieval fantasy quest doesn't obviously relate to field roles. HR asks "apa hubungannya dengan kerja operator?" (what does this have to do with an operator's job?). The report and demo need to show which competency each scenario measures.
- **Cost for internal employees:** 60–80 minutes × number of staff is a visible productivity cost that HR must justify to their own management.

## 9. Why HR trials end the deal

1. **The time comparison:** about 60–80 minutes versus the 15–30 minutes of typical alternatives, and only about 60% of that time produces data.
2. **Around 1,000 clicks feels like work,** especially for a busy HR manager who is not the target candidate.
3. **The blank-screen bug hits trials hardest,** because evaluators often open the link twice or share demo accounts.
4. **Problems pile up:** visible typos and art mismatches make buyers doubt the scoring quality too.
5. **The value comes too late.** The insightful report, the real "wow" moment for a buyer, only appears after an hour.

### Trial and sales recommendations

- **Give buyers a short demo mode:** about 10–12 minutes, with a few story scenes, 4–5 ranking questions and one minigame, a visible skip button, and the evaluator's own demo report at the end.
- **Give each evaluator their own demo account** until the two-session bug is fixed.
- **Lead with proof:** completion rates, average completion time, mobile compatibility, which competencies each part measures, and any local validation data.
- **Offer a pilot with real candidates** (for example 20–30 people) instead of HR self-trials, and review the results together.
- **Follow up within a day** with a 20-minute walkthrough of the evaluator's report.
- **Position it as one assessment covering three areas:** personality, judgement and cognitive ability together, if the scoring supports that. "35 minutes replaces three tests" is an argument HR can take to their management.

## 10. Improvement plan (keeping all 32 ranking questions)

The 32 ranking questions set a minimum of about 20 minutes, even at a brisk 35–40 seconds each. Everything else must be cut hard, and every ranking must feel fast and meaningful.

### 10.1 Cut the story to about a quarter

- **Strict budget:** no more than 2–3 lines of story between ranking questions, about 80 lines total instead of about 300. Delete any line that doesn't set up the next choice or pay off the last one.
- **Summary cards for transitions:** replace travel sequences with one card, e.g. "Two days later, the party reaches Skyreach Mountain."
- **It's writing work, not code:** the story runs on ink, so writers can cut lines in the .ink files without touching game code.
- **Estimated saving:** 20–25 minutes.

### 10.2 Make every interaction faster

- **One click per line:** one click finishes a typing line, the next advances. Speed up the typing effect or show text instantly.
- **Skip button** always visible. Story is unscored, so skipping costs nothing.
- **Tap-to-rank instead of dragging:** tap options from most to least likely, numbers fill in automatically, tap again to undo. Faster, mobile-friendly, no failed drags. Saves about 5–8 minutes across 32 questions.
- **Click anywhere to continue** on every screen, including objective screens.
- **Shorter answer options:** no more than about 15 words each. Explain the "(brackets = thoughts)" convention once.

### 10.3 Make choices matter (cheaply, without hurting scoring)

- Always use the rank-1 option as the hero's next line.
- Add one short reaction line per possible rank-1 choice, spoken by a companion. Use these in place of generic narration so total length still goes down.
- Delete narration where the hero acts against the candidate's style.
- Keep all reactions neutral. No praise for particular answers.

### 10.4 Structure and pacing

- **Five chapters** based on the existing five objectives, about 6 questions each, with a minigame between chapters.
- **Visible progress:** e.g. "Chapter 2 of 5 · about 6 min left".
- **An early minigame:** there's currently none in the first 40%.
- **First ranking question within about 60 seconds** of starting.

### 10.5 Technical fixes

- **Two sessions:** detect a second session and show "This account is open elsewhere — continue here?" Validate story data before showing a scene, and show a friendly error if it's empty.
- **Minigame timers:** pause on connection loss or a hidden tab, or allow a fair retry. Start timers only after the tutorial is closed.
- **After a reconnect:** re-enable ranking input.
- **Loading:** convert WAV audio to compressed formats, load later chapters in the background, and show a tip or story card while loading.
- **Mobile:** test properly on budget Android phones. Remove the "best on PC" message once mobile is solid.
- **Engine:** update the ink runtime to match the story files.

### 10.6 Minigames

- **Harder memory test:** add more symbols, or make it get harder as the candidate succeeds, to avoid everyone scoring 100%.
- **One instruction screen per minigame,** with a short animated example, instead of five Next screens.
- **Shorter timers** if validation allows (2–3 minutes each saves another 4–6 minutes).
- **Link outcomes to the story:** "The box opened with ease" versus "…after a struggle".
- **Check the score mapping** (see the Visual Perception concern in section 7).

### 10.7 Audience fit

- Review the Bahasa version, especially the word puzzle.
- Replace culturally foreign items such as Halloween.
- Use shorter sentences, and consider optional Bahasa voice-over.

### 10.8 Polish and quality checks

- Run a spell-check over the ink files and add it to the build process.
- Add an automatic check that every speaker tag has matching artwork.
- Fix all items in Appendices B–D.
- Move the end survey after the results, or reduce it to two quick inline questions.

### 10.9 Measure it

Add analytics for time per line, time per question, skip rate and drop-off point. This shows exactly where people get bored and gives real completion data to show prospects.

### Time budget with all 32 questions kept

| Part | Now | After changes |
|---|---|---|
| Story | 25–35 min | 5–6 min |
| 32 rankings | 20–30 min | 16–21 min (tap-to-rank) |
| Minigames + instructions | ~14 min | 8–13 min |
| Loading | 1–2 min | under 1 min |
| **Total** | **60–80 min** | **~30–40 min** |

Even fully optimised, the game won't beat a 15-minute test on time. It can win on experience, faking resistance and breadth of insight.

## 11. Ambitious redesign: making it a "wow" game

The goal is to turn "reading a story with a questionnaire inside it" into "a game the player is steering, where the 32 questions are the moments that steer it." The current story, characters and most of the art can be reused.

### 11.1 Core idea: leading an expedition

- **A journey map instead of a stream of dialogue.** Show the five regions (the five objectives) as a map travelled node by node. Each node is an event card (a ranking question), a trial (a minigame) or a camp scene. Players always see where they are and how much is left.
- **Event cards instead of text walls.** Each scenario becomes one card: one illustration, one or two lines of setup, then four answer cards to rank. Mobile players already know this format from games like *Reigns*.
- **Companions react, and the reactions stick.** After each answer, a companion reacts with a short line and an expression change. At camp between regions, the party's mood and conversations reflect how they've been led. The player feels their leadership style shaping the group, which is exactly what's being measured.
- **Minigames as the big moments of each region.** The rune memory test unlocks the Keep, the block puzzle repairs a collapsing bridge, the word puzzle unseals the library. Each gets a short intro, a dramatic success or fail animation, and a visible effect on the map.

### 11.2 Make it feel good to play

- **Satisfying feedback on every action:** cards that flip and snap into ranks, a sound on each placement, a small burst on minigame streaks, smooth transitions, characters that blink and change expression.
- **Music per region and sound on interactions.** Optional short Bahasa voice-over for key lines.
- **A strong first three minutes:** a 20-second animated opening, the first choice within a minute, and a minigame within five.

### 11.3 The results reveal as the climax

- **"Hero class" reveal:** e.g. "You are The Strategist", with custom artwork, a one-line description, a radar chart of traits and the companions who "trusted you most". Make it a card the candidate can save or share.
- **Instant HR view in the demo:** right after the reveal, show the HR dashboard with the evaluator's own profile, which competency each event measured, and a sample comparison across 100 candidates.

### 11.4 Nusantara setting (bold version-2 option)

A world inspired by Indonesian islands and folklore (archipelago kingdoms, guardian creatures, wayang-influenced art touches) would feel fresh, locally proud and memorable in demos. It is expensive and needs care around religious and ethnic sensitivities, so it's best done as a reskin after the new core gameplay is built.

### 11.5 Scoring risks to manage

1. **Game outcomes can bias answers.** If one option visibly earns more resources, morale or a better ending, players will rank for the game rather than answering honestly. Consequences must be balanced (every option has an upside and a cost) or purely for flavour. Never show "correct!".
2. **Changing the context around a question can shift responses,** even with identical wording. Run a comparison study (a few hundred people on old versus new) to check that score distributions and reliability match.
3. **Branching stories multiply content work.** Use "branch and merge": short reaction branches that rejoin the main path, so every candidate still sees the same 32 questions in the same order.
4. **Fun can't mean longer.** Every new game element must replace story time. Target 30–40 minutes.
5. **No real time pressure on ranking questions** unless the psychologists re-check them. Urgent-looking visuals without a real timer are fine.

### 11.6 Where to invest

| Idea | Wow factor | Cost | Scoring risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satisfying feel (animation, sound, transitions) | High | Low–Medium | None |
| Event cards + tap-to-rank | High | Medium | Low (wording unchanged) |
| Journey map with chapters | High | Medium | None |
| Hero-class reveal + HR dashboard | Very high | Medium | None |
| Companion reactions and camp scenes | Medium–High | Medium | Low (keep them neutral) |
| Minigames as region climaxes, harder as you improve | Medium | Medium | Needs re-checking |
| Bahasa voice-over | Medium | Medium | None |
| Nusantara setting | Very high | High | Low–Medium (comparison study) |

With a limited budget, the first four rows give most of the effect.

## 13. Prototype and upgraded screen mockups

  1. **Chapter map** with sealed stops, a 5-chapter progress bar, time left and scepter pieces collected.
  2. **Decision screen** with tap-to-rank seals, a "3rd most likely" prompt and a Confirm button that stays disabled until all four are ranked.
  3. **Dialogue** with Fast text, Auto and Skip scene controls, tap-anywhere to continue, and a companion trust toast.
  4. **Rune Lock** with a streak plaque, 5 runes, clear Different/Same buttons and a timer-pause note.
  5. **Chapter complete** recap with the item found, time taken, party trust, the next chapter's length and a "Save and rest" option.
  6. **Results** with a hero-class banner, aptitude scores labelled by the minigame they came from, a "Save hero card" button and optional star feedback instead of a popup.

  Character figures and scene backgrounds in the mockups are placeholders for the game's existing sprites and painted backgrounds. Trait and aptitude numbers come from the reviewer's run; Spatial is shown as [score] because that run was invalid, and "Strategic" is a guess at a label that was hidden behind the survey popup.
