## Corrections to the recap and earlier steps (founder answers, 2026-10-28)
These override what Steps 1–12 assumed. A rerun must apply them.
1. **DEUS did not buy the Phase 11 games.** The recap says DEUS "acquired more games from Agate" (Recap, Phase 11). The founder says DEUS is Agate's **sales partner** for Next Chapter (cognitive, neuroscience, simulation): Agate does not sell it itself, and **four other companies** have the same partner role. No purchase cost. Conflict to note: Agate's own deck shows a price list and "Book Your Live Demo" (Agate deck, Pricing Structure), and Agate launched it publicly with free pilots (Step 4, W8); how leads are shared between Agate and its five partners is unknown.
2. **The Telkom buyer was Telkom's Assessment Center**, the unit that tests all Telkom recruits, not Telkom itself. The deal came **through Agate**: the Assessment Center contacted Agate for a gamified assessment and Agate brought DEUS in. It did not come from DEUS's own outreach.
3. **Why Telkom stopped:** no third year because Telkom's strategy for its MT intake changed. Telkom could not introduce DEUS to similar groups.
4. **Why BCG churned:** poor service, not lack of need. A lot of bugs and DEUS "weren't able to deliver", and BCG felt abandoned on communication once Wulan handled the account. BCG does not want to come back.
5. **The dad is the funder** (main funder and shareholder) and says he is fine continuing to support DEUS. The founder has the final say. Cash and runway: the founder says the dad can support; the bank balance is still not known.
6. **Bu Sovie is DEUS's data person, not a psychometrician** (founder, 2026-10-28, later the same day). The recap only says she "replaced Rizkie", whose role was psychometrics (Recap, Phase 9 and Reference: people), and earlier steps assumed she took that role. She works on **DEUS Enhance client data**: data cleaning and the manual parts of running Enhance. So **nobody on the team today has psychometric or psychologist skills**: a validation plan, a validity note, a screening-set design or a psychologist network has no owner inside DEUS, and Bu Sovie is a second key person (after Ko Will) for keeping the DKB service running.

## Founder answers (2026-10-28, to the Steps 1–5 questions)
The founder's words, lightly cleaned up. They are the founder's account; buyers have not been asked (the Step 6c interviews have not run).

**Step 1**
- DKB used to ask for many features. Power BI is no longer in use, and DKB still adds more, such as its own KPI groupings and a year-end summary.
- DKB is now more satisfied. BCG left because DEUS couldn't deliver (a lot of bugs) and partly because of Wulan, who largely abandoned them on communication. The Telkom deal came because Telkom contacted Agate for a gamified assessment and Agate pulled DEUS in.
- DEUS did not buy the new games. DEUS is Agate's sales partner for Next Chapter; Agate can't sell it themselves, and there are four other partners like DEUS.
- DKB does not pay extra for the features it asks for; it sees the Rp35 juta as already covering them.
- The dad is the funder and says he is fine continuing to support DEUS.

**Step 2**
- Agate does not sell the games; it partners with DEUS (and others) to sell them.
- Only the cognitive games have a validation; Discover (the personality game) has none yet.
- When deals don't go through, buyers usually say "we'll discuss internally" and then go silent, say they don't need it now, or say the game's experience isn't very good (a bit long, small text), or give personal-preference reasons.
- Potentia was free to acquire; DEUS expanded the report.
- Validation is not complete for all modules.
- F&B prospects take very long, then usually say they already have an assessment, "we'll discuss", go silent, or the deal dies.
- Question from the founder: if DEUS runs Enhance as a DKB service contract, is that still fine as a business model?

**Step 3**
- Agate introduced DEUS to Telkom. Telkom couldn't introduce DEUS to similar groups.
- DEUS has tried cold outreach to similar organisations, but can't make them respond.
- The other logos on the clients page are companies inside the bigger paying groups (e.g. Pepper Lunch under Boga), so all of them are paid.

**Step 4**
- Cash in bank and runway are not a problem: the dad can support.
- The current focus is mainly selling Discover.
- The Enhance server costs about Rp4 juta per month.
- Telkom hasn't renewed for a third year because its MT intake strategy changed.
- DKB doesn't usually accept a written scope with a price per change request; it is used to getting extras free, but does pay Rp35 juta per month.

**Step 5 and later**
- Apollo: without a meeting, prospects usually say "send a proposal" and then go quiet.
- Finance outreach: most don't reply.
- DKB has about 300–400 Enhance users and is quite satisfied now. BCG churned mainly because it felt it got bad service, especially once Wulan handled it, and doesn't want to come back.
- Agate does not sell Next Chapter directly; it sells through DEUS (and the other partners).
- Why people left: Bu Vera resigned; Fizka because of his father's health; Bu Retno because she was replaced with Yess; Fitri because DEUS wanted to combine the data and sales roles; Wulan for poor performance (one of the reasons BCG failed).
- The dad is the main funder and a shareholder; the founder has the final say.
- No success metrics were set for Bu Vera, Fizka, Bu Retno or Yess.
- DEUS rarely uses Discover or Enhance on its own candidates or team.
- If Ko Will left next month, keeping Enhance running would be very hard.
- How many state-owned groups run their own MT intake: unknown. The Telkom deal was with Telkom's Assessment Center, which tests all Telkom recruits, not with Telkom itself.
- The people who signed and decided at DKB, BCG and Boga were HR heads or owners.
- SPH's deal was small, about Rp12 juta.
- Audrey runs the F&B outreach. BCG doesn't want to use DEUS again; Boga already has its own systems.
- Many companies DEUS reaches are curious about Discover but already have their own assessments.
- The other Next Chapter partners have closed deals, the founder thinks, but they took a while and were small: about 100 people or so (founder, in chat, 2026-10-28). Whether that is per deal or in total, how many deals, at what price, and whether any client bought again: ?
