## The verdict in one paragraph

**PIVOT, narrowed: DKB pays the base, one Discover bet gets two to three months with dated gates, and DEUS shrinks to the DKB service if nothing passes.** DEUS as it runs today is not working: neither product has product-market fit (Step 5), every channel DEUS ran itself has closed no deal (Fact sheet, Funnel), all revenue is one client, and the company loses about Rp17–31 juta a month (Fact sheet, Money). The founder's answers made two things clearer. **Enhance has been refused on price, not tested on need**: the dad's contacts say "no budget" at Rp30 juta a month, 2.5–4 times what packaged HR systems list for a company of DKB's size (Founder answers; Step 7, MC6). And **Discover's one big buyer came through Agate's door**, not DEUS's, while four other partners now sell the same Next Chapter modules and DEUS's own personality game has no validation (Corrections 1–2; Fact sheet, Validation). So DEUS changes now. It **keeps and secures the DKB contract** (the contract read, Ko Will backed up, a named account owner). It **stops** Potentia, Apollo, cold lists, new builds, hires and, unless the lease makes it cost more than it saves, the office. It runs **one growth bet**: Discover sold as a service to central assessment units, through Agate and introductions. The bet needs a route by **2026-11-11** (Agate's written lead rule or five named introducers), two programme-owner meetings with dated intakes by **2026-11-27**, and a paid first stage of a dated intake or a signed order by **2026-12-28**, a date that moves to **2027-01-26** only if Agate has passed DEUS a real lead. Beside it, **five founder calls** ask the dad's "no budget" contacts what Enhance would have to cost; only if two name a price above its cost plus a margin does Enhance get a money test. If nothing passes, **DEUS becomes a company sized to DKB's fee**. By **2026-11-04** the founder and the dad sign one page with these dates, what a fail means and who would stay, so open-ended family money cannot quietly move them.

### The founder's three questions, in short (full answers in section 9)

1. **Is Enhance as a DKB service contract a fine business model?** Yes as a contract, and as the base that pays most of the bills, once its scope, terms and key person are secured. No as the whole company at five people: it covers about 53–67% of the monthly costs. Sized to its fee (about three people, no office) it is about break-even, one client and one engineer away from zero.
2. **Is DEUS still worth continuing, and in what way?** Yes, for two to three months, in a narrower shape, with the end already written down: the DKB service as the base, one Discover bet, one cheap Enhance price check, everything else stopped. Not as today's three-product company sold by cold outreach on open-ended family money.
3. **What to do to make it profitable?** Cut what buys no test (the office alone cuts the gap to about Rp5–19 juta a month), secure DKB's Rp35 juta, then add contracts whose margin is known. Margins are NO DATA today, so the next 30 days measure the two numbers that decide it: what one assessment intake costs to run and what the DKB contract costs to serve. The fallback, the DKB-sized company, is about break-even.

3. **Add contracts whose margin is known.** This is where the first pass's arithmetic needs care: "one deal closes the gap" counts revenue, not what is left after costs (Step 7, C14).
