## The Agate route won't repeat

**The founder is right that Agate will stay neutral, and the point goes further.** When Telkom's Assessment Center asked Agate in Phase 7, it bought **DEUS's own personality game**. That was before Next Chapter and its five sales partners existed (Step 3, W04; Step 4). DEUS was then the only company selling an assessment Agate had built, so sending Telkom to DEUS was close to automatic (interpretation). That situation no longer exists. Now that Agate is neutral among five partners selling the same modules, Agate can be a source of occasional leads, but DEUS can't plan on it.

Keep three requests to Agate that don't ask it to take sides:
- **Deal registration:** a group DEUS brings in stays with DEUS.
- **A listing on Agate's partner page** with the Telkom case, so when a buyer chooses between partners, the case is there to see.
- **The routing rule in writing:** rotation, region or the buyer's own choice.

**What Telkom actually teaches:** the buyer was already looking for something new and went to the name it knew (Step 3). Agate is still that name: it is publishing Indonesian articles on game-based assessment [W9]. The channels that work are the ones the buyer already trusts, or ones that are visible while the buyer is searching. Next Chapter is identical across six sellers (five partners plus Agate's own team; Step 6b). DEUS's own game is the only thing that is DEUS's, so the channels that sell it don't depend on Agate at all.

## The alternatives, best first

### 1. Telkom's Assessment Center as a reseller, not just a past buyer
The strongest option.
- **The evidence:** ACI's 2024 catalogue says it serves Telkom Group and also government agencies, the Ministry of SOEs, schools, and private and foreign companies. The Ministry uses it for fit-and-proper tests of candidates for SOE director roles [W1].
- **The gap:** its services are assessor-led assessment centres, development and consulting. There is **no online, high-volume screening** in the catalogue [W1]. That is exactly what DEUS ran for it for two years (Recap, Phase 7).
- **The pitch:** "Offer the first-stage screening we ran for you to your outside clients. You keep the client and the assessor stage; we run the game stage at a wholesale price per candidate."
- **Why it suits DEUS:** this is the one place where DEUS isn't one of six identical sellers. If ACI wanted Next Chapter, it would have to buy through a partner anyway, and DEUS is the partner it already knows (Correction 1).
- **What could kill it:** ACI sees DEUS as a rival, or the unvalidated personality game doesn't satisfy its psychologists (Fact sheet, Validation).
- **Caveat:** that ACI is the unit DEUS dealt with is an ASSUMPTION (Step 6b). This isn't the referral Telkom couldn't give (Correction 3): it's a resale where ACI earns the margin.

### 2. A university psychology institute: validation partner first, then seller
- **The evidence:** LPTUI (UI's applied psychology institute) ran a ministry selection for more than 20,000 participants in 2025 [W4]. In July 2026 Mekari Talenta built its recruitment assessments with LPTUI [W2, W3].
- **What that shows:** these institutes run mass selection and license their tests into other companies' products. Agate did the same thing for Next Chapter with a research institute (Step 4, W8).
- **The pitch:** co-run the validation study of DEUS's personality game, led by the institute because nobody in DEUS has psychometric skills (Correction 6), and the institute then offers the game in its own selection work. That one deal fixes DEUS's biggest weakness, the missing validation (Fact sheet, Validation), and gives DEUS a seller trusted by state groups and government. Their tests are the traditional kind (one is 570 questions and 150 minutes [W3]), so a game is something they don't already have.
- **What could kill it:** academic timelines, a demand for IP ownership, or no past results data to validate on (NO DATA). Fix "long, small text" (Founder answers) before pitching them.

### 3. Psychology bureaus as white-label resellers
- **Why:** they are the "we already have an assessment" that blocks DEUS, so selling through them turns the most common objection into a channel (Fact sheet, Funnel).
- **The pitch:** a wholesale price per candidate, under their brand; DEUS runs the platform and candidate support.
- **A rule to check:** Indonesia's psychology code of ethics reportedly requires psychological assessment to be done by licensed psychologists [W7]. If that covers hiring tests, bureaus are the gatekeepers by rule, which is another reason to go through them rather than around them. Unverified; ask the institute or a licensed psychologist, since nobody in DEUS is one (Correction 6).
- **The test:** five calls, aiming for one paid pilot on one of their client's intakes.
- **The risks:** a margin split, and the four other partners can call the same bureaus (Correction 1).

### 4. The assessment-centre community: PASSTI and the Intipesan event
- **Who's there:** ACI's catalogue says ACI is active in PASSTI, the Indonesian assessment-centre association [W1]. PASSTI's national congress brings together the people who run assessment centres and the people who use them [W5]. That is DEUS's buyers and resellers in one room. Agate launched Next Chapter at "The 7th Assessment Centre by Intipesan" in June 2026 (Step 4, W8).
- **The move:** a joint talk with ACI on the Telkom MT case. A joint talk is worth far more than a booth.
- **The unknowns:** PASSTI's current activity (the latest source is 2019) and costs are NO DATA. Ask ACI to introduce DEUS.

### 5. Be the name an active searcher finds
- **The gap:** in a web search for Indonesian game-based recruitment assessment (2026-10-28), DEUS showed up only on a startup directory, while Agate filled the rest of the page.
- **The move:** one Indonesian case page (the MT intake: 1,000+ candidates, 30+ provinces, results in one day; Fact sheet) once DEUS has permission to name the client, plus founder posts on LinkedIn.
- **Why:** it's slow but costs almost nothing, and a buyer who finds DEUS directly never goes through Agate's routing.

## Lower on the list
- **Recruitment platforms (Mekari Talenta and similar).** The Mekari–LPTUI deal proves the channel exists in Indonesia [W2], so this is "not yet" rather than "no." The platform picked a university institute for credibility, so the way in is probably through #2, after validation.
- **Campus career centres.** UGM's alumni office has hosted companies' psychological tests for campus hiring [W6], but the evidence is old. Worth one call at most.
- **Staffing and outsourcing firms for frontline hiring.** Price per candidate is probably too high for frontline screening (ASSUMPTION), and it's the F&B segment that already said no (Fact sheet, Funnel).
- **State companies' joint recruitment (FHCI).** The central stage is a tender DEUS can't win. The company-level psychological test after it (Step 6b, W1) is run by units like ACI and the bureaus, so #1 and #3 reach it.
- **Cold outreach.** Cold outreach to Telkom-like groups got no response (Founder answers; Fact sheet, Funnel).

## Key findings

1. **The Agate route was a one-off.** Telkom came when DEUS was the only seller of an Agate-built assessment; with five neutral partners, plan on deal registration and a partner listing, not on leads.
2. **Telkom's Assessment Center (ACI) is the best next door:** it sells assessment to outside clients and has no high-volume online screening in its catalogue, the stage DEUS ran for it for two years.
3. **Validation and channel can be one deal:** a university psychology institute such as LPTUI runs mass selection and licenses tests to platforms; co-validating DEUS's game could make it a seller.
4. **Sell through the people who "already have an assessment"** (bureaus, assessment centres) instead of trying to replace them.
5. **DEUS's own game is the only thing that is DEUS's,** so every channel above depends on fixing its validation and its "long, small text" problem.
