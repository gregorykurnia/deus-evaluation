# Indonesian assessment benchmarks: why they sell and DEUS doesn't

As of 2026-10-28. For the founder and the founder's dad; internal. **This is not a step report.** It saves, at the founder's request, Claude's chat answers of 2026-10-28 to: "What do successful Indonesian assessment companies do that we don't? What makes companies buy an assessment or not? Why do they sell tens of thousands while after Telkom we can't sell one? Why do prospects ghost after the meeting, trial and proposal?" Corrections 1 (Agate is DEUS's game developer and DEUS is one of its five Next Chapter partners, not a competitor) and 6 (Bu Sovie is Enhance's data person; nobody on the team is a psychologist) are applied. Where this differs from the Step 8 verdict, the verdict is the considered call.

Sources: `inputs/deus-fact-sheet.md` ("Fact sheet"; "Correction N"), `inputs/DEUS_Company_History_Recap.pdf` ("Recap, Phase N"), `inputs/Agate_Next_Chapter_GBA_deck.pdf` ("Agate deck"), `inputs/DEUS_GBA_Presentation_8.pdf` ("GBA deck"), the step reports ("Step N"), the other plans, and the web sources [W1]–[W30] at the end, all opened on 2026-10-28. **ASSUMPTION** = not in the files or sources; **NO DATA** = number missing; **derived** = arithmetic on cited numbers; **self-claim** = a company's own statement. Everything about DEUS's buyers is the founder's account: the Step 6c interviews have not run.

## The answer

**The successful Indonesian assessment companies are psychology businesses that use technology; DEUS is a technology business selling psychology without a psychologist.** Buyers buy an assessment when it lowers the HR person's risk (a licensed psychologist owns the result), fills a step and a budget that already exist, and comes from a vendor procurement can approve. The incumbents' tests *are* that step, so they are bought again every month without a new decision. DEUS sells a new layer that needs a new decision, a new budget line and trust in an unvalidated game from a five-person firm. Telkom's Assessment Center bought because it was the one buyer that brought the missing pieces itself (interpretation).

The one concrete reason prospects give, that the game is "long, boring, not mobile-optimised", is mostly about **DEUS's own personality game** (Fact sheet, "Why deals don't close"), while Next Chapter, which DEUS already sells, has its own personality module of about 18–20 minutes (Agate deck).

## 2. What makes a company buy

| Driver | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **The test fills a step already in the process.** Hiring already includes a psychology test (psikotes) battery by level: IST, Kraepelin, Wartegg, DAP/BAUM, then DISC for supervisors and MBTI for managers [W23]. Vendors win by putting that same step online. Talentlytica's PAULINE is an online Pauli test, with a reported correlation above 0.6 with paper (self-claim) [W7] | BIPI proposal; Talentlytica |
| **A psychologist owns the result.** Tenders ask for psychologists by name: one expert psychologist plus 14 psychologists or assessors (Setneg) [W24]; 5 feedbackers with the professional psychologist degree (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan) [W25]. Ikutpsikotes charges Rp2.5 juta for a psychologist's summary of 10 candidates, Rp250,000 each (derived) [W8]. If a hire goes wrong, HR can say a licensed psychologist assessed them (interpretation) | Tenders; Ikutpsikotes |
| **The price sits in a band buyers recognise** | Section 3 |
| **Procurement can say yes:** approved-vendor (rekanan) registration on the buyer's own portal; business code (one tender asked for KBLI 70209, management consulting); experience letters; a named team's CVs; past similar contracts worth at least 50% of the budget [W24, W25, W26] | Setneg and BPJS Ketenagakerjaan documents |
| **A trigger:** a hiring wave; a transformation programme (Pos Indonesia [W5]); a cheating scandal (stand-in test-takers in the 2023 joint state-company recruitment [W28]); a rule (director assessment only by Ministry-appointed institutions [W12, W22]) | As cited |
| **An affiliation:** a state company may appoint another state company or affiliate directly, without a tender (PER-2/MBU/3/2023, Article 155(2)(j)); the competition authority KPPU asked for the clause to be removed [W29] | Suits Telkom's Assessment Center |

## 3. Prices: where DEUS sits

| Band | Price per candidate | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Online platforms | About Rp25,000–160,000. Ikutpsikotes: Rp799,000 for 5 (Rp159,800 each), Rp5.5 juta for 100 (Rp55,000), Rp36 juta for 1,000 (Rp36,000), Rp50 juta for 2,000 (Rp25,000) (derived) | [W8] |
| Psychology bureaus, with psychologist's report and usually an interview | About Rp250,000–1.1 juta by level (staff Rp250,000–450,000) | [W9, W19, W23, W30] |
| Assessment centres | About Rp0.75–7.5 juta per person; the state BKN tariff is Rp3.3–7.5 juta for competency assessment | [W30, W13] |
| **DEUS** | List Rp69,000–279,000 by modules and volume; minimum order Rp10 juta; Telkom paid Rp380,000 | Fact sheet, Pricing; Telkom row |

**Interpretation.** DEUS is stuck between bands. At volume it costs about twice the online platforms (Rp69,000 against Rp36,000 at 1,000 candidates). It is cheaper than a bureau, but the bureau price includes the psychologist's report, which is what buyers pay for. So DEUS is neither the cheap option nor the safe one. Telkom's Rp380,000 sits inside the bureau band. DEUS's four modules take 3 h 20 min (Pitch deck, slide 7), about the length of a classic paper battery (IST about 90 min, Pauli 60 min, plus personality and drawing tests) [W23, W31], so it saves no candidate time.

## 4. Why they sell tens of thousands and DEUS can't sell one

1. **Volume comes from hundreds of small repeat accounts, not big deals.** Ikutpsikotes claims 250,000+ test-takers across 900+ companies, about 280 per company (self-claims, derived) [W8]. Its entry package, Rp799,000 for 5, is small enough for an HR officer to approve without procurement (interpretation). Talentlytica built 460+ clients over about nine years [W1]. DEUS's Telkom deal, 1,000 candidates a year, was bigger than their average account. **The gap is the number of accounts and how often they buy again, not deal size.**
2. **DEUS is stuck between price bands** (section 3).
3. **Telkom's Assessment Center was the exception that proves the rule.** It was "specifically looking for gamification vendors" (Recap, Phase 7), came through Agate (Correction 2), and has its own psychologists and assessors: tens of thousands assessed and Ministry director assessments [W10, W11]. **It brought the piece DEUS lacks**; every other prospect needs DEUS to bring it (interpretation).
4. **"Game-based" doesn't set DEUS apart for big buyers.** DDI Indonesia built Pertamina's game [W27]; Agate's page credits its Level Up brand with a Telkomsel culture assessment for 7,000+ candidates and names no partner [W32]; Talentlytica has published a game-based test paper [W1]; Next Chapter is sold by five partners (Correction 1). Who sold the Telkomsel assessment is an open question for Agate's lead and deal-registration rules, not evidence of competition.

## 5. Why prospects go quiet after the meeting, trial and proposal

**What the founder reports** (Fact sheet, "Why deals don't close", added 2026-10-28): the HR head and the decision-maker are usually in the meeting and the proposal is walked through with them; prospects take the trial and read the proposal, then the deal very rarely goes through. Stated reasons: "we'll think about it", "no budget", "we'll discuss later", and from some, that the gamified assessment is long and boring, not mobile-optimised and a poor user experience, mostly about **DEUS's own personality game**.

**Interpretation, most likely first:**
1. **The offer doesn't beat what they have.** The decision-maker weighs a familiar, psychologist-signed, budgeted test with past results against a new vendor with no psychologist, no validation, the same candidate time and an awkward price. "Keep what we have" wins. That this happens with the decision-maker in the room is the strongest sign that the offer, not the selling, is what needs fixing.
2. **The trial loses the deal.** A game's only edge over a cheaper familiar test is candidate experience. If the trial is long, boring and hard on a phone, it removes the one advantage the presentation promised. "Long, small text" was already a known loss reason (Fact sheet), and Step 5 put game length and text size ahead of more selling.
3. **Silence is the "no".** Many Indonesian decision-makers avoid saying no to someone's face, especially after a good presentation (ASSUMPTION). "No budget" usually means "not worth a budget": every prospect already pays for a test.
4. **The decision-maker's questions have weak answers:** "Who else like us uses it?" (Telkom, which stopped); "Who's the psychologist?" (nobody); "Is it validated?" (the cognitive module, through Agate; the personality game, no); "What does it replace?" (usually nothing).
5. **No deadline:** without a hiring round, "not now" costs nothing.
6. **Some meetings are price research** for leverage with the current vendor (ASSUMPTION).
7. **Procurement stops it quietly:** no rekanan registration, a missing document, or an amount that needs a tender [W24, W25].

**The personality game.** Next Chapter's personality module is Big Five-based, 32 items in an RPG-style story with Indonesian settings, about 18–20 minutes; its cognitive set is 7 mini-games of 10–30 minutes (Agate deck). A full screening could be about 30–50 minutes of Next Chapter (derived). The trials have probably shown prospects DEUS's weakest product first (interpretation). The Rp1 billion spent on DEUS's own game is not a reason to keep showing it (CLAUDE.md, rule 3).

## Recommendation

In order:

1. **Stop putting DEUS's own personality game in trials and proposals now.** Lead with Next Chapter's personality and cognitive modules, on a device known to work well. **Caveat, later on 2026-10-28:** the founder finds Agate Level Up's personality module time-consuming too; time it before it goes into a trial (`plans/why-trials-lose.md`, recommendation 3).
2. **Check three things with Agate before the next trial:** whether Next Chapter is properly mobile-friendly (the deck doesn't say); whether its personality module is validated or only "calibrated" with Anvesana's psychologists; what a 30–45-minute screening set should contain.
3. **Bring in a licensed psychologist who owns the service**, part-time or through a partner bureau that signs the reports. It closes the legal gap, meets what tenders score, and lets DEUS price in the bureau band. Ikutpsikotes shows the model: an inexpensive test plus a paid psychologist summary.
4. **Make DEUS's difference the service, not the game:** the Telkom record (1,000+ candidates, 30+ provinces, results in one day), running whole hiring rounds nationwide, the partner psychologist (Step 6b). The game is the same from all five partners, so Agate's lead and deal-registration rules matter more.
5. **Replace a step instead of adding a layer:** a short screening set that takes over one named part of the buyer's battery and is clearly shorter than it. Sell "the same step, online, nationwide, results in one day."
6. **Price in the bureau band, about Rp250,000–450,000 per candidate with the psychologist's report included,** where Telkom's Rp380,000 already sits. Don't try to beat Rp36,000 platforms.
7. **Become procurable:** rekanan registration with each target company; check DEUS's KBLI code against what tenders ask; a reference letter or signed handover document (BAST) from Telkom's Assessment Center as DEUS's strongest similar-experience proof.
8. **Make the proof what HR buys:** results in one day across 30+ provinces, plus the share of people hired through DEUS still employed after a year (NO DATA; ask Telkom's Assessment Center). Candidate enjoyment becomes a side point.
9. **In every meeting, bring the objection out in the room:** "If you were to decide not to go ahead, what would the reason most likely be?" and "What would have to be true for this to replace what you use now?" Write the exact words down. For prospects already quiet: "I assume the timing isn't right, and that's fine. Could you tell me the main reason? One line is enough."
10. **Decide the personality game's future on evidence.** Get Agate's quote for shortening it and making it mobile-friendly, and an institute's quote for validating it. Fund that only if a buyer says they would pay specifically for a DEUS personality module; otherwise retire it from sales by 2026-11-27.
11. **Sell through buyers who bring their own psychologist,** as Telkom did: bureaus, assessment centres, university institutes (`plans/discover-sales-channels.md`, #1–3).

## Key findings

1. **The winners are psychology businesses that use technology;** DEUS sells psychology with no psychologist, which the 2022 law, tenders and buyers all treat as the core of the product.
2. **Buyers buy a step they already have, from someone they can defend;** DEUS sells a new layer, so "we already have one" and "no budget" are rational answers, not excuses.
3. **DEUS is stuck between price bands:** about twice the online platforms at volume, and cheaper than bureaus only by leaving out the psychologist's report.
4. **Volume is many small repeat accounts,** not big deals; Telkom bought because it brought its own psychologists and was already searching.
5. **The trial shows prospects DEUS's weakest product:** the "long, boring, not mobile" complaints are mostly about DEUS's own personality game, while Next Chapter's personality module takes about 18–20 minutes. Lead with Next Chapter and the service.
