## 1. Enhance as a DKB service contract

**Answer: yes, as a small service business that pays its own way, but not at today's size.**

**Facts.** DKB pays Rp35 juta a month; the Enhance server costs about Rp4 juta a month; the office Rp12 juta; staff average Rp8–10 juta each (Fact sheet, Money). DKB asks for features without paying extra and has never accepted a price per change (Founder answers). Keeping Enhance running without Ko Will would be very hard (Fact sheet, Team).

| Per month (Rp juta, derived) | |
|---|---|
| DKB pays | 35 |
| minus the server | 31 |
| minus the office | 19 |

The Rp19 juta left pays about two people at Rp8–10 juta each, so DKB alone covers Ko Will plus roughly one more person. It does not cover the current team of five.

**Interpretation.** It is fragile in three ways: one client is all the revenue, one engineer runs it, and the work keeps growing at no extra price.

**Recommendation.** Four things would make it sound:
- **A written contract** with a notice period.
- **A monthly change allowance:** a set number of days a month included in the Rp35 juta (the number is to be agreed; NO DATA), with anything beyond waiting for next quarter. DKB has refused a price per change, but an allowance gives the same limit in a form DKB may accept.
- **Clear code ownership** (NO DATA; Fact sheet gap 4).
- **A second person** who can fix and deploy Enhance.

On those terms it is a sound base that pays its own way while the founder pushes Discover. It is not a growth business.

## 2. Is DEUS still worth continuing?

**Answer: yes, but not in its current shape.** It is worth continuing as a small company that DKB mostly pays for, with one clear way to grow and a date to decide. It is not worth continuing as three products sold by cold outreach to companies that already have an assessment, on family money with no end date.

### What the founder's answers improved
- **Neither loss means the product doesn't work.** BCG left over bugs and poor service, and Enhance has since been rebuilt (Correction 4; Recap, Phase 10). Telkom stopped because its own MT strategy changed (Correction 3). Both are the founder's account; the buyers have not been asked.
- **The downside is small and covered.** DKB pays Rp35 juta of roughly Rp52–66 juta a month in costs, and the dad will cover the rest (Fact sheet, Money; Correction 5).
- **Real demand has shown up once.** Telkom's Assessment Center went looking for a gamified assessment (Correction 2).

### What they showed is weaker
- **The new games give DEUS no edge over Agate's other partners.** Four other companies sell the same Next Chapter games (Correction 1). The only part only DEUS sells is its personality game, which has no validation (Fact sheet, Validation), and buyers say it is long with small text (Founder answers).
- **DEUS's own selling has not landed a big Discover deal.** The one big deal came from Agate's referral (Correction 2; Fact sheet, Funnel).
- **The most common "no" is "we already have an assessment"** (Fact sheet, "Why deals don't close"). Interpretation: DEUS is asking buyers to replace something, not fill a gap, which is a much harder sale.
- **Everything rests on DKB and Ko Will** (Fact sheet, Revenue by client and Team).

### What "profitable" takes

| Per month (Rp juta) | |
|---|---|
| Costs: team 40–50, office 12, server about 4 | about 52–66 (the server may already be in the founder's estimate) |
| Revenue (DKB) | 35 |
| **Gap** | **about 17–31** (derived) |

Any one of these closes the gap (all derived from the Fact sheet):
- **One more Enhance client at the Rp30 juta quote.** That is about break-even (Rp65 juta in against Rp56–66 juta out). Without the office, it is Rp11–21 juta profit a month.
- **About Rp200–370 juta a year of Discover deals** (Rp17–31 juta × 12). That is one Telkom-size intake (Rp380 juta), or 20–37 orders at the Rp10 juta minimum order. This is before Agate's share and delivery costs, both NO DATA.

So becoming profitable does not need a breakthrough, just **one more paying client**. Growth is a separate goal on top of that.

### The uncomfortable part
- **The founder's focus is Discover, which pays nothing today** (Fact sheet, Team; Revenue by client). DEUS's own outreach has not sold it since Telkom (Fact sheet, Funnel). Meanwhile the product that pays every bill is the one the founder says they can't sell (Founder answers). The question to answer honestly: is Discover the focus because the evidence points there, or because it was the original idea and cost about Rp1 billion (Recap, Phase 1)? Money already spent is not a reason to keep going (CLAUDE.md, rule 3).
- **The dad's open-ended support is generous, and it also lets the company drift.** Nobody ever has to choose (Step 1, Loop 5). 15 of 19 hires left (Recap, Reference: people), and the four the founder named never had success measures (Fact sheet, Team). The fix is not less support but support in stages, tied to results.
- **DEUS rarely uses its own products on its own hires** (Fact sheet, Team). An assessment company in that position has no good answer when a buyer asks "do you use it?".

### What to do from here, in order

**1. Protect what pays (next 2 weeks)**
- **DKB:** a written contract with a notice period; a monthly change allowance instead of a price per change; find out who owns the Enhance code.
- **Ko Will:** a written guide to running and fixing Enhance, and one outside engineer named who could step in.
- **DKB's users:** a short survey, and ask DKB's HR head what Enhance does for them. Their answers become the Enhance pitch.

**2. Stop what doesn't sell**
- Zero hours on Potentia. It was free to acquire (Fact sheet, Money), so stopping costs nothing.
- Stop Apollo and cold outreach to F&B and finance (Fact sheet, Funnel).
- Check the office lease: Rp12 juta is the biggest cost that can be cut without losing anyone (lease terms NO DATA).
- No new builds or hires until a client pays for them.

**3. Enhance: find a price that sells (60 days)**
The dad's contacts say "no budget", not "no need" (Founder answers). That is a price and scope question, and calls can test it. Rp30 juta a month is the same for every company: for a group of DKB's size (300–400 users) it is about Rp75–100k per user a month (derived), and for a smaller company much more. Go back to 5–10 of them and ask what it would need to cost, and for what. Then offer a standard version (DKB's setup without the custom features) at a price set beforehand, as a paid pilot. One yes makes DEUS profitable (section above).

**4. Discover: sell where demand already exists**
- **Agate first.** It is the only channel that has produced a big deal (Correction 2). Get the partner terms in writing: DEUS's share, pricing, exclusivity (NO DATA; Fact sheet gap 2). Ask how Agate decides which of its five partners gets a lead, and why it picked DEUS for Telkom. Aim to be the partner Agate gives the big accounts to.
- **Telkom's Assessment Center.** It tests all Telkom recruits, not just MT (Correction 2), so ask whether another intake could use DEUS. Ask permission to use the case by name (NO DATA).
- **Pitch the delivery, not the games.** Lead with 1,000+ candidates in 30+ provinces with results in a day (Fact sheet, Revenue by client). Interpretation: volume hiring is where "we already have an assessment" is weakest.
- **Worth five calls (ASSUMPTION, untested):** offer Discover to assessment providers and psychology bureaus as a tool. They are the ones who "already have an assessment", so selling through them sidesteps the main objection.

**5. Fix the proof (mostly the team's time)**
- **Validate the personality game.** An outside psychologist or university institute could start from past Telkom and Boga results (nobody in the team has psychometric skills; Correction 6), if that data and candidates' consent exist (ASSUMPTION; NO DATA). Until then, drop "validated" for the personality game in the decks.
- **Track how often "too long, small text" comes up.** If it is in most losses, ask Agate what a fix would cost.
- **Use DEUS's own products:** Discover on the next hire, Enhance for the team's own targets.
- **Fix the website wording** to "6 client groups, 40+ brands", which is what it is (Fact sheet, Public claims).

**6. Run it on numbers**
- One weekly sheet: cash, every lead, and the reason for every "no".
- A 90-day result for each person to be judged on.
- With the dad, agree the next stage's amount and what has to be true before the next one, such as a second paying client. The founder keeps the final say (Correction 5); the milestones keep both honest.

### When to decide
**Proposed (a judgement, not from the files):** a checkpoint at the end of January 2027 and a decision by the end of March 2027. The first-pass verdict picked 2026-12-27 (Step 8, first pass). With funding now settled (Correction 5), the extra three months give Discover buyers one budget cycle, and they cost about Rp50–95 juta (derived: 3 × Rp17–31 juta). If by March neither product has a second paying client, DEUS shrinks to the DKB service sized to its fee and sells Discover only for Agate commission, with no fixed cost. If one product passes, that becomes the company.

**Stop sooner** if DKB gives notice, Ko Will leaves with no backup in place, or Agate drops DEUS as a partner.

## 3. Reasons to end DEUS

### Reasons that would justify ending
1. **No buyer type has proven repeatable.** Since Telkom, no Discover or Enhance deal has come from any channel DEUS ran itself: F&B about 10 meetings and no deals; Apollo no meetings; the dad's network "no budget" (Fact sheet, Funnel; Founder answers). The one big Discover deal came through Agate (Correction 2). If the next few months add nothing from any channel, the market has answered.
2. **What DEUS sells may not be enough to build a business on.** Discover's validated games belong to Agate and four other partners sell them; DEUS's own game has no validation and buyers call it long with small text (Correction 1; Fact sheet, Validation; Founder answers). Enhance is a system shaped around one client (Recap, Phase 3). If Agate's terms or the price test show no margin after delivery costs, it is a weak business even when it sells.
3. **The loss has no end date.** It is about Rp200–370 juta a year (derived). The dad is willing to keep covering it (Correction 5), which is generous. But if nothing changes, that is money and years going into something that never has to prove itself.
4. **The founder's time could be worth more elsewhere.** Only the founder can answer this. A useful test: if the dad offered the same money tomorrow for anything the founder chose, would the founder choose DEUS? If the honest answer is no, and the reason for staying is mainly the years already in, that is a reason to end.
5. **It starts hurting relationships.** DKB came through the dad's network and the dad is the funder (Recap, Phase 3; Correction 5). If continuing starts to strain either relationship, that is a cost the numbers don't show.

### Triggers that should start the ending conversation
- **DKB gives notice** and there is no second client. Revenue goes to zero.
- **Ko Will leaves** before anyone else can run Enhance.
- **Agate drops DEUS** or sends its leads to the other four partners. (Agate dropped DEUS once before; Recap, Phase 4.)
- **The Enhance price test finds no one** willing to pay a price that covers its cost.
- **The validation shows the personality game doesn't predict** job performance.
- **The decision date passes with no second paying client,** and the founder doesn't want to run a small service company.

### Reasons that sound good but aren't
- **"We've already spent Rp1 billion and years."** Money already spent is no reason to stop, just as it is no reason to keep going (CLAUDE.md, rule 3).
- **A bad month or one lost deal.** Judge it on the decision date, not on a mood.
- **"It'll never be big."** A small, profitable company is a real outcome.
- **The people who left.** Several left for reasons that had nothing to do with the strategy: Fizka for his father's health, Bu Vera resigned (Fact sheet, Team).

### What ending could look like, short of closing
- **Shrink to the DKB service:** Ko Will plus one person, sized to the Rp35 juta (section 1). The founder is then free to do something else.
- **Hand Enhance and the DKB contract to Ko Will,** if DKB's contract allows it (NO DATA). DKB keeps its system and Ko Will keeps a business.
- **Keep Discover only as Agate commission sales,** with no staff or fixed cost.
- **A full close done properly:** fair notice to the team, DKB's data handed over cleanly, and things settled with Agate and Potentia's buyers.

### The read
Today the case for ending **now** is weaker than the case for running the cheap tests first: DKB pays most of the costs, the gap is one client, and the tests cost phone calls, not new builds. The real end-or-continue decision comes on the decision date. If neither product has a second paying client by then, either shrink to the DKB service or end. Which one depends on whether the founder wants to run that smaller company, and that is the founder's call, not the analysis's. The first-pass verdict rejected stopping for now and kept it as the call if DKB leaves before a test passes (Step 8, first pass, section 5).

## 4. A big outreach sales team?

**Answer: no, not a big sales team yet. One very good seller, yes, once there is a clear offer for them to sell.**

### Why not a big team now
- **A sales team can scale a sale that already works. It can't find one.** DEUS doesn't yet know who buys, at what price, or why (Steps 3, 5). More sellers would mostly multiply today's results: more "we already have an assessment", more "send a proposal" followed by silence (Fact sheet, "Why deals don't close"; Funnel).
- **Effort wasn't the problem.** Apollo reached hundreds of contacts and got zero meetings; F&B got about 10 meetings and no deals (Fact sheet, Funnel). Interpretation: a better seller gets more meetings, but those reasons are about the offer and who is targeted, so the meetings would mostly end the same way.
- **It repeats the pattern that got DEUS here.** Building before knowing who would buy was the main mistake the analysis found (Step 1, Loop 5; Step 11, reason 2). Hiring a sales team before knowing what sells is the same mistake with people.
- **DEUS has tried "more sales hires" before.** Seven sales or account roles over the years, none filled by an experienced seller (Step 12, citing Step 6a), and the four the founder named had no success measures (Fact sheet, Team). Spending more on bigger names without fixing that repeats it at a higher price.
- **The money and time don't add up yet.** Big-company HR deals take months; the F&B prospects took "very, very long" (Founder answers). A team could burn through several months of the dad's money before DEUS learns whether it works. Senior sellers' salaries are NO DATA; ASSUMPTION: they cost well above the team's Rp8–10 juta average, so each one widens the Rp17–31 juta monthly gap.

### What to do instead
1. **The founder sells the new offers in person for the next 4–6 weeks:** the Enhance price calls, Agate, and Telkom's Assessment Center. The first conversations about a new offer need the founder in the room to hear the objections first-hand.
2. **Then hire one experienced seller, not a team:**
   - **Track record:** has sold to HR heads or owners at large Indonesian groups, for example in assessment, HR software or a psychology bureau. Their contacts are a large part of what DEUS is hiring.
   - **Target:** a 90-day goal measured in money (paid pilots or signed deals), not meetings.
   - **Pay:** a modest base and a large commission, so the cost rises only when sales do.
   - **Material:** one target segment, one price and the Telkom case to sell with.
3. **Or try a commission-only agent first,** if someone who already sells into HR departments can be found. It costs almost nothing until they close.

### When to go hard
Build a sales team when **all four** are true:
- **Wins:** two or three recent paying wins in one segment, from a channel DEUS can repeat. The dad's introductions and a single Agate referral don't count.
- **Margin:** DEUS knows the price that sells and what is left after Agate's share and delivery costs.
- **Reasons:** DEUS knows why buyers say yes, and has an answer to the most common "no".
- **Repeatable:** the one seller has closed deals without the founder in the room, and delivery can handle more. Ko Will and Bu Sovie are each the only person in their role (Fact sheet, Team; Step 6a).

At that point, spending the dad's money on sellers is backing something proven. Before that, it is a bet on hope.

## Key findings

1. **Continue, but not as DEUS runs today:** a small company DKB mostly pays for, one clear way to grow, and a decision date (proposed: checkpoint end of January 2027, decision end of March 2027).
2. **Profit needs one more paying client:** one Enhance client at Rp30 juta a month is about break-even, or about Rp200–370 juta a year of Discover deals before Agate's share and delivery costs (derived; Fact sheet).
3. **The biggest new weakness is Discover's edge:** its validated games are Agate's and four other partners sell them; DEUS's own game has no validation. The one big deal came through Agate, so Agate is the channel to work first (Corrections 1–2).
4. **Ending now is weaker than testing first.** The real decision comes on the decision date; shrink or end then depends on whether the founder wants to run a DKB-sized service company. DKB's notice, Ko Will leaving without a backup, or Agate dropping DEUS would bring it forward.
5. **No big sales team yet:** the founder sells the new offers for 4–6 weeks, then one experienced seller on commission-heavy pay; a team only when wins, margin, reasons and repeatability are all known.
