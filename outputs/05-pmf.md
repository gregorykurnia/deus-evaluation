## The answer in one paragraph

**Neither product has product-market fit, and the founder's answers make the reasons clearer, not the verdict softer.** Discover's best evidence is still one buyer, Telkom's Assessment Center, which paid for two yearly MT intakes. It now has a recorded reason for stopping (Telkom changed its MT intake strategy) and a recorded source: **it asked Agate, and Agate brought DEUS in** (Corrections 2–3). So the one proof point shows that a buyer with an open search will pay well, not that DEUS can find one. No Discover buyer pays today, and every channel DEUS ran itself has converted zero (Fact sheet, Funnel). The founder's account of why prospects say no, "we already have an assessment" most often, then a long game with small text, then "we'll discuss internally" and silence, points to one gap in the channels tried so far: **DEUS has been selling to buyers who have no open search** (rung ① of the skill's ladder). For the narrower segment Step 4 backs, assessment units, two other gaps weigh as much: the personality game DEUS owns has no validation, and four other Agate partners sell the same modules, while Agate's own launch page has its business-development team running pilots directly (rung ③ and "why DEUS"; W8). Enhance has a different shape: **one account that stays and is "quite satisfied" on the founder's account (DKB, about 300–400 employees using it, Rp35 juta a month) and a market that has said no** at Rp30 juta a month (the dad's contacts: "no budget"; Apollo: "send a proposal", then silence). BCG, the one client that left, left over service (bugs, delivery, communication), not lack of need, on the founder's account (Correction 4). That is a retained account, held by the relationship or the product (unknown which), not product-market fit, and it supports Step 4: run Enhance as a DKB service contract and stop selling it to the market. The 30-day plan replaces "ask Telkom why" (now answered) with four cheaper questions: Agate's partner terms and leads, the reasons lost prospects give in their own words, how deeply DKB uses Enhance and whether its users would miss it, and what the DKB contract really costs. It also has a result that would end the narrow Discover bet. Decision date: 2026-11-27.

## 4. Fit segment analysis

- **Discover:** the only account that ever repeated is **a central assessment unit of a large group with a high-volume MT intake (1,000+ candidates, Telkom plus 6 subsidiaries, 30+ provinces, results in 1 day) that went looking for a gamified assessment and asked Agate** (W04; Pitch deck; Corrections 2–3). It lasted as long as the programme: when Telkom changed its MT intake strategy, the buying stopped. The account that did not retain bought an internal assessment of existing staff at a low price and now has its own systems (W01). The segments tried since (F&B, finance, the dad's retail network, cold Telkom-like lists) had no open search, and the reason prospects give most, "we already have an assessment", fits that reading, though it also fits ③ (section 3.1; Founder answers). What Telkom praised was a **service-led MT assessment project** run by a team most of whom have since left (Bu Retno, Wulan and Sahid left; Audrey remains; who delivered it: NO DATA; Recap, Phases 7–8, Reference: people), using the single personality game, not today's 3 h 20 min suite (Pitch deck, slide 7). Fit, if it exists, lives in **buyers with an open search, reached through the vendor they ask**. In Phase 7 that vendor was Agate, before Agate launched Next Chapter as its own product (Recap, Phase 11; W8, June 2026). Today Agate sells Next Chapter through five partners on the founder's account (Correction 1), its launch page has its own business-development team scoping pilots (W8), and it has run a Telkomsel assessment itself (W4). Whether Agate would pass DEUS an open-search lead now is unverified.
- **Enhance:** the account that retained (DKB: 300–400 employees with access, "quite satisfied" on the founder's account) came through the dad and gets features built on request (Step 3; Fact sheet); whether the relationship or the product holds it is unknown. The account that left (BCG) had the need and left over service, on the founder's account (Correction 4). Neither describes a market segment; both describe custom-system clients of a family network.

**Interpretation.** The pattern is the same as Step 3's: buyers who paid had a dated, structured HR process and a problem already in hand (an MT intake; a complex KPI and appraisal system), and came through someone else's door. Neither product has shown pull from anyone without that.
