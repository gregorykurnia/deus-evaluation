## The answer in one paragraph

DEUS has one buyer type with real evidence behind it, and DEUS did not find it itself. The only repeat Discover buyer was **Telkom's Assessment Center: a central unit that tests every recruit for a large group, running a high-volume management-trainee (MT) intake, that went looking for a gamified assessment and asked Agate, which brought DEUS in** (1,000+ candidates, Telkom plus 6 subsidiaries, 30+ provinces, two years in a row, Rp380,000 per candidate in the second year; Recap, Phase 7; Pitch deck; Corrections 2–3). It stopped after two years because Telkom changed its MT intake strategy, and it could not introduce DEUS to similar groups (Correction 3). Every buyer who paid a large amount (Telkom's Assessment Center, DKB, BCG) arrived with a need it already had, and the two that repeated came through someone else's door: Agate and the dad. Every channel DEUS ran itself (the dad's wider network, MoM partners, recognition programmes, F&B, finance and Apollo outreach, and cold outreach to organisations like Telkom) produced no deal (Fact sheet, Funnel; Founder answers). The most common reason prospects give for not buying is that **they already have an assessment** (Fact sheet, Funnel). So the profile is now sharper and the channel problem is worse: the buyer DEUS wants is an assessment unit with an open search, and those buyers go to a vendor they already know (here Agate, which now has four other partners for its own suite; Correction 1), not to a cold email. The 11 logos on the website are, the founder says, companies inside about six paying groups, not separate buyers (Founder answers). For Enhance there is still no ICP: one satisfied client tied to the family, one that churned over poor service. Buyers' own words are still almost absent, so most lines below remain Assumption until the interviews run.

## 4. Do Discover and Enhance have different buyers?

Yes. Discover's evidenced buyer is a **central assessment unit with a yearly volume hiring event** (W04). Enhance's buyers are **groups with complex KPI and pay systems that want a system built around them**, signed by an HR head or the owner (W02, W03; Founder answers). The trigger, the budget line (recruitment vs performance and compensation) and the decision-maker differ. No buyer has bought both products (Fact sheet, Revenue by client). **Interpretation:** the site's "One partner to hire, develop and pay" pitch (deushcs.com) to the same company is not supported by any purchase so far.

Potentia's buyers are schools (W05; deuspotentia.com targets "parents and schools", deushcs.com). Two of the six buyers are schools (W05, W06), each about Rp12 juta, and SPH bought Discover, not Potentia (Fact sheet). That is a separate, low-value segment, not a third ICP to chase now (Step 2 recommends pausing Potentia).

## Key findings

1. **The one evidenced Discover buyer is a central assessment unit with an open search, and it reached DEUS through Agate.** Telkom's Assessment Center asked Agate for a gamified assessment, ran DEUS for two years at up to Rp380,000 per candidate, then stopped when Telkom's MT intake strategy changed (W04, C03; Recap, Phase 7; Corrections 2–3). It is the best profile DEUS has, it rests on one account, and its channel belonged to Agate, which now has four other partners (Correction 1).
2. **Buyers with an existing need paid, and both repeat buyers came through someone else's door.** Telkom's Assessment Center (via Agate) and DKB (via the dad) arrived with the problem in hand. Every channel DEUS ran itself, including cold outreach to Telkom-like organisations, produced no deal (W02, W04; L01–L09; Founder answers).
3. **The strongest anti-profile signal is "we already have an assessment".** It is the most common reason prospects give for not buying (founder's account; Fact sheet, Funnel). Telkom's Assessment Center also had testing in place, but it was asking for something new. The open search is the signal, and it is not visible from outside.
4. **The website's breadth is brands, not buyers.** The 11 logos and "40+ brands" are, on the founder's account, brands inside about six paying groups (Founder answers). They make ten industries look like proof; outreach was spread on that picture.
5. **Enhance has one satisfied custom-system client, not an ICP.** DKB (300–400 users, "quite satisfied", unpaid features) stays through the relationship; BCG had the need and left over poor service; cold outreach got zero meetings and the dad's connections say no budget at Rp30 juta (W02, C01, L02, L07; Correction 4). Buyers' own words for any of this are still missing: the interviews in section 8 are what would turn these lines into evidence.
