The skill's engagement files (precision anchor, validated research) do not exist in this project, so the seven key questions in PLAYBOOK.md section 4 are used as the question the analysis must answer, and Steps 1–6c as the research to test.

## The answer in one paragraph

The analysis asks the right questions about the right company, but it can only **partly** answer them, because two things every conclusion needs are missing. **The customer's voice is missing:** no buyer, churned client or lost prospect has said in their own words why they bought, stayed, left or said no; the only buyer-side words are four short quotes DEUS chose for its Telkom slide (GBA deck). **The cash is missing:** the founder does not know the bank balance (Fact sheet). Until the Step 6c interviews run, every conclusion about who buys and why is DEUS's own reading of events. Within that limit the conclusions fall into three groups. **Strong enough to act on now:** the cheap, reversible moves every step agrees on, which hold whatever the interviews say (no new builds, zero hours on Potentia, stop Apollo and untargeted network waves, find out the cash and Agate's terms, make the Telkom, F&B and DKB calls, correct the "40+ brands" and validity claims). **Act on only as a test with a pass line:** the Telkom-shaped buyer profile, the state-group beachhead, the "we run your MT screening" positioning and the founder in the selling seat, each resting on one lapsed account or one quote. **Too weak to use as evidence:** "Telkom paid above list price" (it bought a single game at 2.4–5.5 times today's list for one module), "Telkom came without a relationship" (how Telkom, or BCG, found DEUS is unknown) and "one good deal a year closes the cash gap" (revenue, not margin). The strongest counter-case is not against narrowing but against *which* product: Enhance has about twice Discover's recorded revenue and two of two qualified buyers paid, so the Discover-over-Enhance call is closer than Steps 2–5 say. **Verdict: PARTIAL. Proceed to Step 8, carrying these qualifications into the verdict (section 11).**

## How to read this report

- **Facts** are cited. **Interpretation** is marked. Recommendations are in sections 5 and 11. Anything not in the files is labelled **ASSUMPTION**; missing numbers are **NO DATA**; **derived** marks arithmetic on cited numbers, shown in full.
- Each claim gets one verdict: **ACT** = strong enough to act on now (the evidence holds and the move is cheap and reversible); **TEST** = act on it only as a test with a pass/fail line set in advance; **HOLD** = too weak to use as evidence in Step 8.
- **Evidence strength** (strong, medium, weak) is a judgement; the reason is in the row. **Independent sources** counts source types that come from neither the founder nor DEUS's marketing (section 1). Steps that cite each other count once.
- Hypotheses H1–H5 are Step 6c's; measures M1–M7 are Step 5's 30-day plan.

## 4. Key vulnerabilities

1. **Everything about Discover rests on one account the files cannot see into.** Telkom is one buyer with two deals, a recalled price, an unknown lead source, an unknown reason for no third year, a delivery team that has mostly left, a group that now hires through the joint process, and three rivals that name Telkom-group clients (C3, C5–C7, C12; Step 4, W3, W4). A skeptical reader would say: "You've built a beachhead, a positioning and a 90-day plan on one phone number." The reports' own answer, the Telkom call first, is the right one; Step 8 must not treat the profile as more than a hypothesis.
2. **The Discover-over-Enhance call rests on a difference the data doesn't show.** Step 4 backs Discover as "the only product with evidence that a buyer with no relationship pays a large amount" (Step 4, section 4). But Telkom's route to DEUS is NO DATA, so is BCG's, and Enhance has the larger revenue record (MC3). In Phase 11 neither product's cold outreach produced a recorded deal (F&B 0; finance NO DATA; Apollo 0 meetings; Fact sheet). Both cases are open.
3. **No verdict can be time-bound without the cash.** Every plan has a clock and no runway to set it against. If family money covers the gap, the clock is not real and the loops keep running (Step 1, Loop 5; ASSUMPTION). If it does not, a beachhead that buys through state procurement may pay after the money runs out (E5).

## 5. Steel-man counter-narrative

The strongest case against the emerging conclusion ("narrow to Discover for Telkom-like MT buyers, run Enhance for DKB's cash, build nothing"), written as its advocate would:

> DEUS's proven business is Enhance, not Discover. Two of two companies that came with a complex KPI and appraisal problem paid Rp35–40 juta a month, one while the product was still in development (Recap, Phase 3; Fact sheet). Enhance has earned about Rp900 juta or more (BCG about Rp480 juta, DKB about Rp420 juta in the last twelve months alone), about twice Discover's recorded Rp440 juta (MC3). DKB stayed through years of bugs, which says the need is real. The rebuilt Enhance passed "a clear audit" (Recap, Phase 10): for the first time DEUS has a clean version, and it has never been offered properly to qualified buyers. An Apollo campaign to unnamed contacts is no test of a system worth about Rp420–480 juta a year that has always sold through senior relationships, and senior access is exactly the dad's strength (Step 6a). Discover, by contrast, earns nothing today; its one repeat buyer has not bought since Phase 7; its games belong to Agate, which now sells them directly, pitches the MT use case on its own deck and offers free pilots (Agate deck; Step 4, W8); and its MT beachhead is being drawn into a central state process (Step 6b, W1). Backing Discover means putting the team's selling time behind the product with no current buyer, no owned content and a supplier competing for the same accounts. Put that time behind Enhance for multi-brand groups with complex KPI and pay systems, introduced by the dad, and keep Discover as an add-on or hand it back to Agate.

A second, shorter case: **shrink to what pays.** DKB's Rp35 juta covers about 3.5–4.4 people at today's average salary, before the office (Step 4, derived). A smaller company running Enhance for DKB without the Rp12 juta office would be close to break-even (derived; ASSUMPTION that it can run that lean), and would stop the monthly loss while the founder tests one product at a time. No step weighs this, or "stop", as a company-level option, although PLAYBOOK question 7 names "stop".

**What would move the call toward the steel-man:** DKB's users answer "very disappointed" at 40% or more (Step 5, M5) and 2 of 10 DKB-like groups agree to a paid pilot at a fixed price (Step 2, Experiment 2); or Telkom says its MT screening now runs only through the joint process and Agate's terms let it sell the same games to every private group. **What would confirm the current call:** Telkom names DEUS's service as the reason it bought and says its next intake is open to an outside vendor, and two programme-owner meetings happen by 2026-11-27 (Step 5, M1).

**Recommendation for Step 8:** decide explicitly whether to test both products in the same 30 days. Step 5 put Enhance's Experiment 2 after 2026-11-27 so the team runs one outbound test at a time. But Experiment 2 needs only ten introductions the dad can make to DKB-like groups and about ten meetings of founder time; testing one product only leaves no evidence on the other if the first fails.

## Key findings

1. **Verdict PARTIAL: the right questions at the right altitude, with two dimensions missing.** No buyer's own reasons are in the evidence (the only buyer-side words are four short quotes DEUS chose for its Telkom slide), and the bank balance is unknown (GBA deck; Fact sheet). Step 8 can proceed, but its verdict stays provisional until the Step 6c interviews run.
2. **Strong enough to act on now: the cheap, reversible moves.** No new builds, zero team hours on Potentia, stop Apollo and untargeted network waves, get the cash figure and Agate's terms, make the Telkom, F&B and DKB calls, show paying buyers only, back or soften the validity claims, and write role cards. These hold whatever the interviews show.
3. **The Telkom-based plan is a test, not a finding, and two of its supports don't hold.** The buyer profile, the state-group beachhead and the "we run your MT screening" positioning rest on one account and one quote. "Telkom paid above list" compares a one-game deal with a four-module list; like for like it paid 2.4–5.5 times list, which says more about what the fee covered than about pricing power. "Telkom came without a relationship" is unknown.
4. **The strongest counter-case is Enhance, not Discover.** Recorded Enhance revenue (about Rp900 juta or more) is about twice Discover's (Rp440 juta plus unknowns), two of two qualified buyers paid, and the rebuilt system's only cold test was an email campaign. The call between them is close and should be tested both ways in the same 30 days; "shrink to what pays" and "stop" also need weighing in Step 8.
5. **Agreement across Steps 1–6c is mostly one account read six ways.** The steps cite each other, so a repeated claim is not a corroborated one; the only independent evidence is about competitors and markets. Risks no step priced: time to cash in state procurement, a burn that leaves out tools the recap names, the delivery cost of a service-led deal, and a supplier that has dropped DEUS once and now competes for the same buyers.
