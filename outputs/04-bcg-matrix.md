## The answer in one paragraph

On the classic matrix all three products sit on the low-share side: DEUS has a handful of buyers in markets where the leaders claim hundreds or thousands (W3, W5, W6). So the matrix does not pick a winner by itself; it says DEUS has **three Question Marks and no Cash Cow**, which is the most dangerous portfolio shape there is (no funding source, every unit consuming cash). Read by cash instead of by market, the picture is sharper: **Enhance-for-DKB is the only unit that brings in cash** (Rp35 juta a month, 100% of revenue; Fact sheet), **Discover is the only unit with evidence it can win a large deal again** (Telkom, Rp380 juta in one deal; Fact sheet), and **Potentia has neither**. The call: **invest in Discover, narrowly** (sales time on Telkom-like volume-hiring buyers, no new builds), **maintain Enhance as a priced service contract for DKB** (no product investment unless a second buyer pays), and **exit Potentia** (zero team hours from now; decide within 90 days whether to sell, hand back or shut the site). This fits the ICP from Step 3: the only evidenced buyer type is Discover's. It also carries a risk the ICP did not show: the large-group buyers in that ICP are exactly where established assessment vendors, and Agate itself, already sell (W3, W4, W8).

## How to read this report

- **Facts** are cited. **Interpretation** is marked. Recommendations are in sections 4 and 5. Anything not in the files or a cited web source is labelled **ASSUMPTION**; missing numbers are **NO DATA**.
- **Derived** marks simple arithmetic on cited numbers (shown in full so it can be checked).
- Web market figures come from paid-report vendors' public summaries (Ken Research, IMARC, Market Research Future). They disagree with each other, their methods are not visible, and none sizes the exact niche DEUS sells into. Treat them as **direction, not measurement**.
- BCG convention: market growth above about 10% a year counts as "high"; relative share is DEUS's size divided by the largest competitor's (above 1.0 = leader).

| The one evidenced buyer type is Discover's: a large group with a yearly, high-volume MT intake, already looking for a gamified vendor (Step 3, key finding 1) | **Supports "invest in Discover"**, and says how: by the buyer's situation, not by industry. | Schools are a separate, low-value segment (Step 3, section 4) | **Supports "exit Potentia".** |
| Telkom paid above list price; price was not the deciding factor (Step 3, section 2) | **Supports selling on fit, not discounts.** Packages of 5–100 candidates (Pitch deck, A8) target a different, smaller buyer than the ICP |

**Where the ICP test raises a risk the matrix hides.** The ICP buyer (large groups with MT programmes) is the segment where competitors are strongest: Talentlytica and Talentics both name Telkom-group companies, state banks and large conglomerates as clients (W3), and Agate, DEUS's own game supplier, now sells the same game-based suite directly with free pilots (W4, W8; Agate deck; Step 2, key finding 3). So "invest in Discover" is only sound if DEUS can answer **why Telkom chose DEUS over them** (NO DATA; Step 3, question 1). If the answer is "price" or "luck", DEUS has no defensible share in its own ICP, and the Discover bet should be smaller and shorter. If the answer is something DEUS controls (its report, its service, its psychometric work), that is what to sell.

## 6. Limits of this analysis

- **The matrix is weak for a company this small.** It assumes share brings cost advantage; with 1–3 buyers per product there is no such effect. BCG's own later work favours speed of learning over share in uncertain markets (skill notes). Here the matrix is used for its cash logic and its "pick one Question Mark" rule, not for its share arithmetic.
- **Market definitions move the dots.** Defined as "custom systems for DKB", Enhance has 100% share and is a Cash Cow; defined as "HRIS in Indonesia", it is invisible. Both readings are shown (section 2.3).
- **Web market figures conflict** (from USD 146 million for HCM software to USD 6.1 billion for a broader "HR technology" report from the same vendor as W1; W1, W1b, W2) and none sizes game-based assessment or kids' assessment in Indonesia.
- **Competitor client counts are their own claims** (W1, W3, W4, W6b) and are used only as an order of magnitude.
- **No customer voice.** Why Telkom chose DEUS, why BCG left and why prospects said no are all NO DATA (Step 3, section 7). The interviews in Step 6c can move every call above.

## Key findings

1. **Three Question Marks and no Cash Cow.** On market data all three products are low-share in markets growing roughly 7–12% a year (W1, W2, W5); the portfolio has no funding source except one client, the shape the matrix calls most dangerous.
2. **By cash, the products are very different.** Enhance-for-DKB is the only revenue (Rp35 juta a month, Fact sheet); Discover is the only product with a buyer who came without a relationship and paid a large amount twice (Telkom; Recap, Phase 7); Potentia has neither (one deal of about Rp12 juta).
3. **One good deal a year closes the gap for Discover or Enhance; Potentia would need 17–27** (derived from the Fact sheet's gap of Rp17–27 juta a month). Only Discover has evidence of where that deal comes from.
4. **Call: invest narrowly in Discover (sales time, no new builds), maintain Enhance as a priced DKB service contract, exit Potentia.** This is consistent with Step 2's per-product calls and Step 3's ICP.
5. **The Discover bet carries a competitive risk the ICP did not show:** established assessment vendors and Agate itself already sell to the same large groups, including the Telkom group (W3, W4, W8). Why Telkom chose DEUS is the single most important unknown for this decision.
