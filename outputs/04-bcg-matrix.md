### 4.1 The founder's question, from the portfolio side: "If I run Enhance as a DKB service contract, is that still fine as a business model?"

Step 2 (section 6) answers it in full: **yes for the contract, no for the company.** The matrix adds three points:

- **It is the right way to run a Dog that pays.** The BCG rule for a unit with no market prospects that still earns cash is to stop investing and collect the cash. A service contract does exactly that: DKB pays for what it uses, and nothing is spent on Enhance as a product. It contributes about Rp31 juta a month after the server, before team time (Fact sheet; derived).
- **It does not fund the portfolio.** Even if DKB took no team time at all, Rp31 juta covers about half to 60% of the burn (derived: 31 ÷ 62 to 31 ÷ 52), and the rest comes from the dad. A business model that needs a funder every month is not yet a business model; it is a bet with a sponsor. The bet it sponsors should be Discover.
- **It is only as safe as two people.** One client is 100% of revenue, and one engineer, Ko Will, is the only person who can keep it running (Fact sheet, Team). A written scope and a documented system are what turn it from a dependency into a contract (Step 2, section 6, conditions 1–4).

If the Discover bet fails its 60-day test, the honest fallback is a much smaller company running Enhance for DKB, not a fourth product.

If it is something DEUS controls (its own game, its report, its large-intake delivery), that is what to sell, and what to tell Agate.

## 6. Limits of this analysis

- **The matrix is weak for a company this small.** It assumes share brings cost advantage; with 1–3 buyers per unit there is no such effect. BCG's own later work favours speed of learning over share in uncertain markets (skill notes). Here the matrix is used for its cash logic and its "pick one Question Mark" rule, not for its share arithmetic.
- **Market definitions move the dots.** Defined as "custom systems for DKB", Enhance has 100% share and is a Cash Cow; defined as "HCM software in Indonesia", it is a Dog. Both readings are shown (section 2.3). Splitting Discover in two is also a definition choice: buyers see one Discover offer with 1–4 modules (GBA deck).
- **Web market figures conflict** (from USD 146 million for HCM software to USD 6.1 billion for a broader "HR technology" report from the same vendor as W1; W1, W1b, W2) and none sizes game-based assessment or kids' assessment in Indonesia. HRIS prices (W10) are list "from" prices.
- **Competitor client counts are their own claims** (W1, W3, W4, W6b) and are used only as an order of magnitude.
- **No customer voice.** The founder's answers explain Telkom, BCG and the lost prospects, but in the founder's words, not the buyers' (Fact sheet, Founder answers). The Step 6c interviews can still move every call above.

## Key findings

1. **Still no Star and no Cash Cow; Enhance is now a Dog as a market product.** Every unit is low-share; Enhance's own market grows below 10% (W2) and nobody has said yes at the Rp30 juta quote, about 2.5–4 times packaged HRIS list prices for a company of DKB's size (Fact sheet, Funnel; W10, derived). The portfolio's real funders are one client and the dad (Correction 5).
2. **Discover is two units, and DEUS owns only one.** The personality game is DEUS's and is what Telkom's Assessment Center bought (Recap, Phases 7, 11); the Next Chapter modules are Agate's, sold by DEUS and four other partners at no purchase cost (Correction 1). The share DEUS can build is in its own game, report and large-intake delivery, not in the modules.
3. **Neither product has won a large buyer through DEUS's own channel.** DKB came through the dad, Telkom's Assessment Center through Agate (Correction 2; Step 3). One Telkom-size deal or one more Enhance client at the quote would close the yearly gap of Rp204–372 juta; Potentia would need 17–31 deals (derived).
4. **Call: back Discover narrowly (sales time on its own parts, through Agate and introductions), carry Next Chapter at no cost, run Enhance as a DKB service contract and stop selling it to the market, exit Potentia.** That answers the founder's question: a DKB service contract is a fine cash line (about Rp31 juta a month after the Rp4 juta server, before team time), not a company.
5. **The Discover bet now rests partly on Agate.** Four other partners sell the same modules, Agate decides who gets each lead, and the terms are not written (Correction 1; Fact sheet, gap 2). Getting those terms in writing is the first move, before more outreach.
