## The answer in one paragraph

**The beachhead stays the Telkom type of buyer, but it is now defined by the buyer unit, not the company: the central assessment unit of a large state-owned group that tests recruits for several companies at once (S1, 54 of 80), with private groups that have the same kind of unit in the same list (S2, 49).** Telkom's buyer was its **Assessment Center**, which asked Agate for a gamified assessment; Agate brought DEUS in; it paid up to Rp380,000 per candidate for two yearly MT intakes and stopped when Telkom changed its MT intake strategy (Corrections 2–3; Fact sheet). Two facts make this beachhead harder than the first pass said. First, **the rivals for these buyers are now Agate's four other Next Chapter partners and Agate's own business-development team, selling the same modules at the same list** (Correction 1; Step 4, W8), so S1's competition score falls to the lowest on the scale and its total from 59 to 54. Second, **an assessment unit always "already has an assessment"**, the reason prospects give most for not buying (Fact sheet, Funnel): the signal that matters is an **open search**, and it reaches the vendor the unit already asks, not a cold email (Step 3). So the beachhead has two routes, Agate's leads (if Agate's written terms say who gets them; Step 5, M4) and introductions, and **two audiences for its positioning: the assessment unit, and Agate, which decides which partner gets a lead.** The point of difference that passes the tests is the one the other four partners cannot claim on present evidence: **DEUS ran Telkom's Assessment Center's MT screening, 1,000+ candidates in 30+ provinces, two years running**, as a service alongside the unit's own assessors (GBA deck). The part only DEUS owns, its personality game, is not yet a difference because it has no validation (Fact sheet, Validation). A new segment is worth one test: **assessment-centre providers that run assessments for other organisations** (S9, 46). Telkom's own unit appears to be one: a 2017 report describes Telkom's Assessment Center Indonesia (ACI) serving state companies, government offices and a university [W7]. **F&B (39), small batches (34) and education (39) stay off the list; finance stays only for firms with a programme and an introduction (47).**

Discover's offer today is DEUS's own personality game plus Agate's Next Chapter modules (cognitive, neuroscience, simulation), 60 + 20 + 30 + 90 minutes for the full suite (Pitch deck, slide 7; Correction 1), priced per candidate (Rp69,000–279,000 list; Fact sheet, Pricing) or in packages from 5 candidates (Pitch deck, A8).

### 2.3 Strategy: concentrated (niche)

With five people, no seller with a track record and revenue of Rp35 juta a month against a burn of about Rp52–62 juta (Fact sheet, Money; Step 6a), the skill's rule is still: **concentrate on one buyer type**. The dad is funding the gap and will keep doing so (Correction 5), which removes the cash deadline but not the choice: a differentiated strategy (separate offers for MT intakes, frontline hiring and small batches) is what the website and price list already attempt, ten industries and packages from 5 to 10,000 candidates (W6; Pitch deck, A8–A9), and it has produced no Discover sale since Telkom (Step 5). How long the beachhead gets is a written funding amount and review date, which Step 6a recommends; it is not a reason to widen it.

It is still better than the alternatives: every other segment has already said no or was never the right shape, and Gate 1 tells the founder within two weeks whether the Agate route exists at all.

## Key findings

1. **The beachhead is a buyer unit, not a company: central assessment units of state-owned groups (S1, 54/80), with private groups' units in the same list (S2, 49/80).** Telkom's buyer was its Assessment Center, reached through Agate, which paid up to Rp380,000 per candidate for two MT intakes and stopped when Telkom's MT strategy changed (Corrections 2–3; Fact sheet). S1 still scores highest, by a smaller margin, and no segment now has high DEUS strength.
2. **Agate's four other partners are now the main rivals in the beachhead.** They sell the same Next Chapter modules at the same list, and Agate's own team pilots them (Correction 1; Step 4, W8). The modules, the features and the cognitive validation are points of parity; S1's competition score falls to 1 of 5.
3. **The point of difference is the Telkom track record plus the service, run alongside the unit's own assessors.** It is the one claim the other partners can't make on present evidence. DEUS's own personality game is the only module no partner has, but it has no validation, so it is a difference in waiting. Because Agate routes leads, DEUS also needs a position in Agate's mind (section 3.5).
4. **An assessment-unit buyer always "already has an assessment", and it is reached through a door, not a list.** The signal is an open search or a programme change, which shows up at Agate or through an introduction; cold outreach to similar units got no response (Founder answers; Step 3). Two dated gates from Step 5 decide the route: Agate's written lead rule by 2026-11-11 and two owner meetings by 2026-11-27.
5. **A new door is worth one conversation: assessment-centre providers (S9, 46/80).** Telkom's unit appears to sell assessment to outside organisations as well [W7] (ASSUMPTION that it is the same unit). F&B (39), small batches (34) and education (39) stay exited; finance stays selective (47).
