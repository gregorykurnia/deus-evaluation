As of 2026-10-28. First pass, no interviews yet. Sources: `inputs/DEUS_Company_History_Recap.pdf` (cited as "Recap, Phase N"), `inputs/deus-fact-sheet.md` ("Fact sheet"), `inputs/DEUS_Pitch.pdf` ("Pitch deck"), `inputs/DEUS_GBA_Presentation_8.pdf` ("GBA deck"), `inputs/Agate_Next_Chapter_GBA_deck.pdf` ("Agate deck"), `outputs/01-systems-thinking.md` to `outputs/06a-mckinsey-7s.md` ("Step 1" to "Step 6a"), and web sources [W1]–[W6] (list at the end; fetched or searched 2026-10-28). Step 4's web sources are cited as "Step 4, W3" and so on. Method: segmentation, targeting and positioning (`stp` skill): segment profiles, the five validity tests, weighted attractiveness scoring, the targeting matrix, a perceptual map, points of parity and difference, and a positioning statement.

## The answer in one paragraph

Segment Discover's market by **the hiring event a buyer has**, not by industry: that is what separated the one repeat buyer from everyone who said no (Step 3). Of eight segments, the beachhead is **state-owned groups that run their own yearly management-trainee (MT) intake for several companies at once**, the segment Telkom belongs to (Telkom plus 6 subsidiaries, 1,000+ candidates, 30+ provinces, two years running; Recap, Phase 7; GBA deck). It scores highest (59 of 80) because it is the only segment where DEUS has a reference, a named contact and a price above list (Rp380,000 per candidate; Fact sheet). It also carries the two biggest risks in this report: the joint state-company recruitment now runs the early screening centrally for 107 companies [W1; Step 4, W7], and Agate sells the same games to the same buyers directly (Agate deck). So the beachhead comes with a switch rule: if the Telkom call in Step 5's plan shows that state-company MT screening has moved into the joint process, the same offer goes to **private groups with their own MT intake** (second, 54 of 80). **F&B leaves the target list (39); finance stays only for firms with an officer-development or MT programme (47), and after the first two.** The positioning cannot rest on the games, because Agate has the same ones: it has to rest on **running the whole MT screening for the buyer, proven at Telkom's scale**. That is the only point of difference that passes the tests, and it is also the one the current five-person team is least proven at delivering (Step 5; Step 6a).

## 1. Segmentation

**Indonesian employers that buy candidate or employee assessment from an outside provider** for hiring, promotion or talent decisions. Discover's offer today is a game-based suite: personality, cognitive, neuroscience and simulation games, 60 + 20 + 30 + 90 minutes (Pitch deck slide 7), priced per candidate (Rp69,000–279,000 list; Fact sheet, Pricing) or in packages from 5 candidates (Pitch deck, A8).

Not in scope: Potentia's buyers (schools and parents for children; Step 4 calls it exit) and Enhance's (performance management; Step 3, section 4: different trigger, budget and buyer).

### 2.3 Strategy: concentrated (niche)

With five people, no seller and a cash gap of about Rp17–27 juta a month (Fact sheet; Step 6a), the skill's rule is clear: **concentrate on one segment**. A differentiated strategy (separate offers for MT intakes, frontline hiring and small batches) is what the current website and price list already attempt: ten industries, packages from 5 to 10,000 candidates, a free trial (deushcs.com [W6]; Pitch deck, A8, A9). It has produced no sale since Telkom (Step 5).

**Why not S2 first?** S2 has no central substitute, which makes it look safer. But DEUS has no buyer, no contact and no loss data there, and a reference only travels well once the referee still buys. The cheapest next deal is the account that already bought twice. Starting with S1 costs one phone call to find out whether it is still alive; the switch rule makes the change to S2 cheap.

**Said plainly.** The beachhead rests on one lapsed account. If Telkom says it moved to the joint process or chose another vendor, this report's first choice falls away, and S2 becomes the bet with no proof at all. That is still better than the alternatives: every other segment has already said no or was never the right shape.

## 4. STP summary and recommendations

### 4.1 Strategy in one paragraph

Concentrate on one buyer: the group HR team that owns a yearly MT intake for several companies, state-owned first (S1, starting with the Telkom group), private next (S2) if the Telkom call shows state companies now screen centrally. Sell a managed MT-screening service, not a game platform, because Agate sells the same games itself. Stop F&B, small-batch and education outreach; take finance only where a firm runs an officer or MT programme. Recommendation, not fact: this follows from one repeat buyer and the absence of any other, and the interviews in Step 6c can overturn it.

## Key findings

1. **Segment by hiring event, not industry.** The only repeat buyer had a yearly MT intake; prospects without a hiring event in hand (F&B, the retail network, the mixed finance list) did not buy (Step 3; Step 5; Recap, Phase 11). Eight segments result; three pass all five validity tests (S1, S2, S3).
2. **Beachhead: state-owned groups running their own yearly MT intake (S1, 59/80), starting with the Telkom group, with private groups (S2, 54/80) as the switch.** S1 has DEUS's only reference, a named contact and a price above list (Fact sheet); the joint state-company recruitment now runs early screening centrally [W1], so a week-1 Telkom call decides whether S1 or S2 leads.
3. **F&B exits (39/80); finance stays only where a programme exists (47/80).** F&B frontline hiring pays about Rp100,000 a head once and produced 0 deals from ~10 meetings; finance firms with an ODP or MT programme are the right event in the most contested territory (Step 4, W3).
4. **DEUS's games are a point of parity, not a difference:** Agate sells the same suite directly, with the same features, from Rp49,999 per user (Agate deck). The only point of difference that passes the tests is running the whole MT screening for the buyer, proven at Telkom's scale.
5. **The positioning's weakest link is delivery, not message:** of the Phase 7 sales and account hires only Audrey remains, who ran the Telkom intakes is unrecorded, and whether five people can run a 1,000-candidate intake today is untested (Step 5; Step 6a).
