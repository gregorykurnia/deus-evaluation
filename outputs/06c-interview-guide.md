# Step 6c: Interview guide: getting the customer's voice

As of 2026-10-28. First pass: no interviews have run yet; this is the guide for running them. Sources: `inputs/DEUS_Company_History_Recap.pdf` (cited as "Recap, Phase N"), `inputs/deus-fact-sheet.md` ("Fact sheet"), `inputs/DEUS_GBA_Presentation_8.pdf` ("GBA deck"), `inputs/DEUS_Pitch.pdf` ("Pitch deck"), `inputs/Agate_Next_Chapter_GBA_deck.pdf` ("Agate deck"), and `outputs/01-systems-thinking.md` to `outputs/06b-stp.md` ("Step 1" to "Step 6b"). Method: `interview-guide` skill (build mode): research brief with testable hypotheses, screener, a 30–45 minute guide built on the switch timeline and the four forces, a note template with a consent line, and a stopping rule. Questions follow the skill's question rules: ask about the last real occasion, never about the future, never pitch.

## The answer in one paragraph

DEUS has **no customer's own words** about why anyone bought, stayed, left or said no (Step 3, finding 5; Fact sheet, Founder notes). The only buyer lines on file are four short quotes on the GBA deck's Telkom slide, and the selection lead's one praises the service, not the tool (Step 5). Every big call so far, from the beachhead (Step 6b) to the DKB service contract (Step 4) to dropping F&B (Step 6b), therefore rests on DEUS's own reading of events. This guide turns those readings into **five hypotheses a buyer can contradict**, and sets up **about 15 interviews in four groups**: Telkom (why it chose DEUS, and what decides a third year), DKB (whether it stays for the product or the relationship), BCG and Boga (why they left or didn't come back), and the ~10 F&B prospects who met DEUS but didn't buy. **The Telkom interview comes first**, because it is the gate in Step 5's 30-day plan (M6) and it decides whether Step 6b's beachhead holds. Two rules matter more than any question: **ask what happened the last time, not what they would do**, and **don't sell during the interview**. The asks (a third year, introductions, permission to use a quote) come after the research part, in the same call but clearly separate.

## How to read this report

- **Facts** are cited. **Interpretation** and **recommendations** are marked. Anything not in the files is labelled **ASSUMPTION**; missing numbers are **NO DATA**.
- The hypotheses (section 1) are DEUS's current beliefs written so an interview can prove them wrong. They are not findings.
- Company names are kept, because the guide targets named accounts and the readers are internal (CLAUDE.md rule 9). People are referred to by role only, as the skill requires.
- Questions are in English (CLAUDE.md rule 8); the interviewer asks them in Bahasa Indonesia, in the buyer's own words.

## 3. The guide

### 3.1 Rules for the interviewer

1. **Ask about the last time it happened**, step by step. Never "would you…", "do you usually…", or "how much would you pay…" (skill question rules QR1, QR6, QR8).
2. **Don't pitch, don't defend.** If the buyer criticises DEUS, ask "what happened then?" and write it down word for word. Don't explain the new games or Ko Will's rebuild during the research part (QR5).
3. **Don't lead.** Not "was it the bugs?" but "what made you decide to stop?" (QR2). The hypotheses in section 1 are for the note-taker, not to be read out.
4. **Write down their words** (verbatim, in Bahasa Indonesia), not a summary. Mark your own summaries "paraphrase".
5. **Silence is a question.** After an answer, wait three seconds before the next question.
6. **Asks come last.** For Telkom and DKB, the commercial asks (a third year, introductions, the users' survey, permission to use a quote) come after the closing question, introduced as a separate topic.
7. **Ask before recording**; say how the notes will be kept and who will see them (the founder, the founder's dad and the analysis files in this repo).

### 3.2 Common structure (30–45 minutes)

| Part | Minutes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Warm-up | 3 | Role, team, what a normal week or an intake season looks like |
| 2 The last time (switch timeline) | 15 | One concrete occasion, walked in order: first thought → passive looking → active looking → decision → first use → today |
| 3 Four forces | 8 | Push, pull, anxiety, habit: whatever the timeline didn't cover |
| 4 Cost | 5 | Money, hours, errors, stress the job costs them now |
| 5 Alternatives | 4 | What else they tried or considered, and why it didn't stick |
| 6 Their words | 3 | "How would you describe DEUS to a colleague who hasn't seen it?" |
| 7 Close | 2 | Anything we should have asked; who else we should talk to (role, not name) |

Sections 3.3–3.6 adapt this to each group. The bracket after each question names the hypothesis or gap it tests.

### 3.3 (a) Telkom: why they chose DEUS, and what decides a third year

**Warm-up**
1. What is your role in the MT programme, and how many people are on the selection team? (context)
2. Walk me through one MT intake season, from opening registration to the final list. (context; Step 6b, the hiring event)

**The last time: the first decision to use DEUS**
3. Think back to before the first intake with DEUS. How did you screen MT candidates then, and what happened that made the team want to change it? (H1; push)
4. When did you start looking for a gamified assessment? What happened that day or that week? (H1; Recap, Phase 7)
5. How did you find DEUS? Who else did you look at, and how did you compare them? (H1; Step 3 data gap: lead source and shortlist; Step 4: Agate, incumbents)
6. Who else was involved in the decision, and what did each of them care about? (who decides; who pays)
7. What almost stopped the decision? (anxiety)
8. Walk me through the first intake with DEUS: the day the candidates played, the day you got the results. What surprised you? (first use; Fact sheet: 30+ provinces, results in 1 day)

**The last time: the second year and after**
9. When you decided to run it with DEUS a second time, how did that decision happen? Who raised it? (why stay)
10. Tell me about the most recent MT intake. How did you screen candidates that time, and who was involved? (H2; Step 6b, W1: the joint state-company recruitment)
11. Where does the joint state-company recruitment fit into your process today, and which stages does Telkom still run itself? (H2; Step 6b data gap)

**Forces (if not covered)**
12. What had to be true for you to pick an outside vendor at all, instead of the in-house test? (push)
13. What did you hope the games would give you that the old way didn't? (pull)
14. What worried you about game-based assessment before the first intake: validity, candidates' reactions, the vendor's size? (anxiety; Step 2 data gap on validation; ask the first part as an open question and only list examples if the buyer is stuck)
15. What did people inside Telkom say who preferred the old way? (habit)

**Cost**
16. What did a full intake cost the selection team in people and days, before DEUS and with DEUS? (cost; don't ask about DEUS's price, it is in DEUS's records)

**Alternatives**
17. Since then, which other assessment vendors have pitched to you or worked with you? What happened? (H1, H2; Agate deck: Agate sells directly; Step 4, W4: Agate's Telkomsel project)

**Their words**
18. If a colleague in another state company asked you about DEUS, what would you tell them? (positioning words; Step 6b)

**Close**
19. Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
20. Which other groups or subsidiaries run an MT programme like yours? Who runs it there (role)? (lookalikes; Step 6b S1/S2 list)

**Asks (separate, after the research part)**
- Is there an MT intake planned for next year, and when is the decision made? (Step 5, M6: third year yes / no / when)
- Would you introduce us to two of the people you named? (M6: 2 named introductions)
- May DEUS use your quote and Telkom's name in an MT case study? (Step 6b, question 1)

## 6. How many interviews, and when to stop

- **Groups (a)–(c): talk to everyone reachable.** One account each; there is no sample to size. One Telkom interview answers H1 and H2 for Telkom only, not for state-owned groups in general.
- **Group (d): aim for at least 7 of the ~10** F&B prospects, which is Step 5's M3 line ("usable reason from ≥7 of 10").
- **Stopping rule (the skill's heuristic):** stop a round when about three interviews in a row add no new reason or force. With about 15 interviews in four different groups, that is unlikely to happen inside a group except F&B.
- **What interviews can't show:** how common a reason is. They show which reasons exist and why. "How many" comes from the outreach log (Step 5, section 8) and the DKB survey (M5), not from these calls.
- Background: Guest, Bunce and Johnson (2006) found most codes within 12 interviews in one homogeneous group on one topic; "five users" comes from usability testing (Nielsen Norman Group, 2000) and doesn't apply here. These are the skill's references, stated as heuristics.

## Key findings

1. **DEUS has never asked a buyer why.** No buyer's own reason for buying, staying, churning or saying no is recorded; the only buyer lines are four short quotes on the GBA deck's Telkom slide (Step 3, finding 5; Step 5; Fact sheet, Founder notes). Every decision so far rests on DEUS's own reading of events.
2. **Five hypotheses carry the strategy, and each can be proven wrong in one call:** Telkom bought the service, not the game (H1); the missing third year is outside DEUS's control (H2); DKB stays for the relationship (H3); BCG and Boga left over quality (H4); F&B was curiosity without a need (H5).
3. **The Telkom interview is the gate.** It decides between Step 6b's beachhead (state groups) and its switch (private groups), and whether DEUS sells the games or the whole MT screening. It belongs in week 1, before the outreach list is contacted (Step 5, M6).
4. **The interviewer is the main risk to the evidence.** The founder is close to every account, and DKB came through the dad's relationship (Recap, Phase 3). A note-taker, verbatim quotes, no pitching, and the asks kept apart from the research part are what make the answers usable.
5. **About 15–17 interviews in four groups, most in the 30-day window.** Telkom 1–2, DKB 2–3, BCG 1, Boga 1, F&B up to 10 (at least 7 for M3). Their answers go into the fact sheet, and Steps 7 to 10 rerun with them.
