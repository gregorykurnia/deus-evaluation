## 3. The guide

### 3.1 Rules for the interviewer

1. **Ask about the last time it happened**, step by step. Never "would you…", "do you usually…", or "how much would you pay…" (skill question rules QR1, QR6, QR8).
2. **Don't pitch, don't defend.** If the person criticises DEUS, ask "what happened then?" and write it down word for word. Don't explain the new games, Ko Will's rebuild or the partner model during the research part (QR5).
3. **Don't lead.** Not "was it the strategy change?" but "what made you decide not to run it again?" (QR2). The hypotheses in section 1 are for the note-taker, not to be read out. The founder already has an account of each reason; the interview is there to hear whether the other side tells it the same way.
4. **Write down their words** (verbatim, in Bahasa Indonesia), not a summary. Mark your own summaries "paraphrase".
5. **Silence is a question.** After an answer, wait three seconds before the next question.
6. **Asks come last.** For Agate, the Assessment Center and DKB, the commercial asks come after the closing question, introduced as a separate topic.
7. **Ask before recording**; say how the notes will be kept and who will see them (the founder, the founder's dad and the analysis files in this repo).

### 3.2 Common structure (30–45 minutes)

| Part | Minutes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Warm-up | 3 | Role, team, what a normal week or an intake season looks like |
| 2 The last time (switch timeline) | 15 | One concrete occasion, walked in order: first thought → passive looking → active looking → decision → first use → today |
| 3 Four forces | 8 | Push, pull, anxiety, habit: whatever the timeline didn't cover |
| 4 Cost | 5 | Money, hours, errors, stress the job costs them now |
| 5 Alternatives | 4 | What else they tried or considered, and why it didn't stick |
| 6 Their words | 3 | "How would you describe DEUS to a colleague who hasn't seen it?" |
| 7 Close | 2 | Anything we should have asked; who else we should talk to (role, not name) |

Sections 3.3–3.7 adapt this to each group. The bracket after each question names the hypothesis or gap it tests.

## 6. How many interviews, and when to stop

- **Groups (a)–(c): talk to everyone reachable.** One organisation each; there is no sample to size. One Assessment Center interview answers H2 and H3 for that center only, not for state-owned groups in general; one Agate meeting answers H1 for Agate's routing, not for the market.
- **Group (d): aim for at least 7 of the ~10** F&B prospects, which is Step 5's M3 line ("a usable reason from ≥7 of 10").
- **Stopping rule (the skill's heuristic):** stop a round when about three interviews in a row add no new reason or force. With about 14–16 interviews in four different groups, that can only happen inside F&B.
- **What interviews can't show:** how common a reason is. They show which reasons exist and why. "How many" comes from the outreach log (Step 5, section 8), Agate's request count, DKB's server logs and the survey (M5), not from these calls.
- Background: Guest, Bunce and Johnson (2006) found most codes within 12 interviews in one homogeneous group on one topic; "five users" comes from usability testing (Nielsen Norman Group, 2000) and doesn't apply here. These are the skill's references, stated as heuristics.
