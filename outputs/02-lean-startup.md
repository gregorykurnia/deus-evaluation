# Step 2: Lean Startup: how the products were built

As of 2026-10-28. First pass, no interviews yet. Sources: `inputs/DEUS_Company_History_Recap.pdf` (cited as "Recap, Phase N"), `inputs/deus-fact-sheet.md` ("Fact sheet"), `inputs/DEUS_Pitch.pdf` ("Pitch deck"), `inputs/DEUS_GBA_Presentation_8.pdf` ("GBA deck"), `inputs/Agate_Next_Chapter_GBA_deck.pdf` ("Agate deck"), `outputs/01-systems-thinking.md` ("Step 1"). Method: Build-Measure-Learn, leap-of-faith assumptions (value and growth hypotheses), innovation accounting, pivot types (`lean-startup` skill).

## The answer in one paragraph

DEUS has run the Build half of Build-Measure-Learn many times and the Measure and Learn halves almost never. Across eleven phases, every product decision was a build or a purchase (a Rp1 billion game, a second product off one client, a year-long digital report, an acquired kids' product, a full Enhance rebuild, a new game suite, a rebrand), and no phase records a test that was set up in advance with a number that would have said "stop". The two moments that did produce real learning, BCG signing for Enhance before it existed (Phase 3) and Telkom buying Discover two years in a row (Phase 7), were not turned into the next hypothesis. Phase 11 repeats the pattern: the games and the rebrand are builds, not tests. The cold outreach is the first genuine experiment DEUS has run, but nobody wrote down beforehand what result would count as a pass. There is also a new fact the recap doesn't mention: the Phase 11 games are Agate's own product, which Agate sells directly to the same buyers (Agate deck). The next 60 days should be spent measuring, not building: three cheap experiments are proposed below, each with a pass/fail line set before it starts.

## How to read this report

- **Value hypothesis:** "the buyer gets enough value to pay, and to pay again." **Growth hypothesis:** "new buyers keep arriving through a channel DEUS can repeat."
- **Facts** are cited to a source. **Interpretation** is marked as such. Anything not in the files is labelled ASSUMPTION; missing numbers are NO DATA.
- Pass/fail thresholds in the experiments are **proposed decision lines**, not data. They are judgement calls the founder can move, but they should be fixed before each test starts.

## 2. Where DEUS built without measuring or learning

### The Build-Measure-Learn ledger

### What the ledger shows

1. **Eleven builds or buys, two closed loops at best.** The eleven: Discover v1 (Phase 1), shortening the game (Phase 4), Enhance v1 (Phase 3), the digital report (Phase 6), the Power BI layer and the parallel Enhance website (Phase 9), the Potentia acquisition (Phase 9), the Enhance rebuild and the Potentia website (Phase 10), the new games and the rebrand (Phase 11). Only Boga's feedback (Phases 2→4, 6) and the Power BI stopgap (Phase 9) show a measured problem leading to a change. Boga's feedback turned into more than a year of building rather than a small test; Power BI was the one cheap, fast response. (Recap, Phases 1–11.)
2. **The best signals were wasted.** BCG pre-buying Enhance and Telkom buying Discover twice are the two strongest value signals in eleven phases. Neither produced a written description of the buyer, a target list of lookalikes, or a price test. This is the core Lean Startup failure in DEUS's history: not the building itself, but not learning from the few experiments that worked.
3. **The measures DEUS used were vanity metrics:** selection into programmes, a booth, MoMs signed (Phases 2, 5), team confidence (Phase 11), and on the public side "40+ brands" (deushcs.com; Fact sheet) against five clients in the recap plus SPH from the founder. Actionable metrics (meeting-to-pilot rate by segment, repeat purchase, reasons lost, dev hours per client) are almost all NO DATA (Fact sheet, Funnel by channel).
4. **Big batches, slow loops.** The Discover report took about a year (Phase 6); Enhance took three to four years and was rebuilt (Phase 10); the vendor switch cost almost a year (Phase 4). Each loop was measured in years, so DEUS learned a few things per product in the whole history. ASSUMPTION: with clear tests, the same period could have run dozens of small loops.
5. **"Build to feel ready" is the mental model behind it** (Step 1, Loop 5). Lean Startup names this exactly: building is used to reduce the founder's uncertainty, when only a buyer's behaviour can reduce the business's uncertainty.

**Said plainly:** the problem was never that the products were bad. Telkom's quotes and the Ko Will rebuild suggest they can be good (GBA deck, Success Story; Recap, Phase 10). The problem is that DEUS spent its money and years answering "can we build it?" when the open question, every time, was "who will pay for it, and how do we reach more of them?". Sunk cost (the Rp1 billion, the four years of Enhance) is not evidence for either product.

## 5. The three cheapest experiments to run next

Each one is planned in reverse, as the method asks: what we need to learn, how we'll measure it, and the least we need to build (which is nothing new in all three).

### Experiment 1: Telkom-lookalike paid pilots (Discover, value and growth)

- **Learn:** do organisations with Telkom's profile (an annual MT, graduate or campus programme with hundreds of candidates, already open to new assessment methods) buy Discover when they are approached directly?
- **Measure:** meetings with the owner of the programme; paid pilots (minimum order Rp10 juta, Pitch deck A9, or the Pilot package).
- **Build:** nothing. A list of 20–30 named accounts (SOEs and their subsidiaries, large groups with MT programmes), the existing Telkom slide and Ibu Lina's quote (GBA deck, Success Story), and one ask to Telkom: will it run a third year, and will it introduce DEUS to two peers?
- **Time and cost:** 45–60 days of founder and Audrey time; no cash beyond the team.
- **Pass:** at least 5 meetings with a programme owner **and** at least 1 paid pilot within 60 days. **Fail:** fewer than 2 meetings, or 0 paid pilots from 20–30 qualified accounts. A pass means this is the beachhead; a fail, together with no third Telkom year, means Telkom was a one-off and Discover needs a bigger pivot (section 4).

### Experiment 2: Enhance fixed-scope offer to 10 DKB-like companies (Enhance, is it a product?)

- **Learn:** will any company other than DKB pay for the rebuilt Enhance as a fixed product at a stated price?
- **Measure:** paid pilots or signed letters of intent at the stated price.
- **Build:** a one-page offer: the core that every client gets, what is extra and at what price, a monthly price. Enhance pricing is not published today (Fact sheet), so setting it is part of the test. A demo of Ko Will's version.
- **Who:** 10 companies that resemble DKB (ASSUMPTION: multi-brand or multi-outlet groups with complex KPI setups; DKB's own profile beyond "Delamibrands" is NO DATA). The dad's network may introduce them, as long as they fit the profile.
- **Time and cost:** 30–45 days; no cash.
- **Pass:** 2 of 10 agree to a paid pilot or sign an intent at the stated price. **Fail:** 0 of 10. Fail confirms the service-contract call in section 4: keep DKB, price its change requests, and put no product-building time into Enhance.

### Experiment 3: Ask the F&B prospects why they didn't buy (Discover, learning from the existing funnel)

- **Learn:** what blocks a prospect who already took a meeting: price, trust in the science, timing, no budget, or no need?
- **Measure:** a reason recorded for each of the ~10 F&B meetings (Fact sheet); number who take the Rp999,000 Pilot package (Pitch deck, A7).
- **Build:** nothing. A five-question call script and a one-line log per prospect.
- **Time and cost:** two weeks of calls; no cash.
- **Pass:** a usable reason from at least 7 of 10, and at least 1 paid pilot. **Fail:** fewer than 5 reachable, or every reason is "no need". The reasons feed Experiment 1's pitch, the Step 3 ICP, and whether the new suite (D6) or the price matters.

## Key findings

1. **DEUS built a lot and measured almost nothing.** Eleven builds or purchases across eleven phases; at most two show a measured problem leading to a change, and no phase records a test with a pass line set in advance (Recap, Phases 1–11).
2. **The best evidence was never used.** BCG paying for Enhance before it existed (Phase 3) and Telkom buying Discover twice at Rp380,000 per candidate (Phase 7; Fact sheet) are the two real validations. Neither became a defined buyer profile or a target list.
3. **Phase 11 repeats the pattern, with a new risk.** The game suite and rebrand are builds, not tests, and the suite answers an objection no lost buyer is recorded as making. The new games are Agate's own product, sold directly by Agate at Rp49,999–300,000 per user (Agate deck), so they give DEUS no edge Agate can't offer itself; Agate's terms with DEUS are NO DATA.
4. **The cold outreach is the right instinct, aimed at the wrong buyers and missing its decision lines.** It is DEUS's first real experiment, but F&B and finance were not chosen from the Telkom evidence, and Enhance via Apollo and the dad's network have already given their answer (zero).
5. **Per product:** persevere on Discover but zoom in on volume-hiring programmes; run Enhance as a paid DKB service contract unless 2 of 10 lookalikes pay; pause Potentia. Three no-cost experiments in 60 days decide each one.
