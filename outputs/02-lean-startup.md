## The answer in one paragraph

DEUS has run the Build half of Build-Measure-Learn many times and the Measure and Learn halves almost never. Across eleven phases, every product decision was a build or an addition (a Rp1 billion game, a second product off one client, a year-long digital report, a free kids' product whose report DEUS then expanded, a full Enhance rebuild, a partner game suite, a rebrand), and no phase records a test that was set up in advance with a number that would have said "stop". The founder's answers change two facts and confirm the pattern. The Phase 11 games were **not bought**: DEUS is one of five sales partners for Agate's Next Chapter (Correction 1). And **both repeat buyers came through someone else's channel**: DKB through the dad, Telkom's Assessment Center through Agate (Correction 2; how BCG arrived is NO DATA). The loss reasons the founder now reports point at buyers who already have an assessment and at the game's usability, not at missing modules (Fact sheet, Funnel). So the next 60 days should be spent measuring, not building: three no-cost experiments below, each with a pass/fail line set before it starts. The founder's question about Enhance gets its own section (section 6): **running Enhance as a DKB service contract is a sound way to run that contract, but it is not a business model for DEUS on its own**: it covers about 56–67% of the monthly burn, rests on one client and one engineer, and its scope is still unpriced.

## How to read this report

- **Value hypothesis:** "the buyer gets enough value to pay, and to pay again." **Growth hypothesis:** "new buyers keep arriving through a channel DEUS can repeat."
- **Facts** are cited to a source. **Interpretation** is marked as such. Anything not in the files is labelled ASSUMPTION; missing numbers are NO DATA.
- The founder's answers are cited as facts about what the founder reports. Where they describe what buyers think (loss reasons, satisfaction), they are the founder's reading until the interviews run.
- Pass/fail thresholds in the experiments are **proposed decision lines**, not data. They are judgement calls the founder can move, but they should be fixed before each test starts.

### What the ledger shows

1. **Eleven builds or additions, two closed loops at best.** The eleven: Discover v1 (Phase 1), shortening the game (Phase 4), Enhance v1 (Phase 3), the digital report (Phase 6), the Power BI layer and the parallel Enhance website (Phase 9), the Potentia acquisition and its expanded report (Phase 9), the Enhance rebuild and the Potentia website (Phase 10), the partner suite and the rebrand (Phase 11). Only Boga's feedback (Phases 2→4, 6) and the Power BI stopgap (Phase 9) show a measured problem leading to a change. Two of the eleven cost no purchase price (Potentia, the partner suite; Founder answers, Correction 1), but each still took team time to learn, expand or sell. (Recap, Phases 1–11.)
2. **The best signals were wasted, and both came through someone else's door.** BCG pre-buying Enhance and Telkom's Assessment Center buying Discover twice are the two strongest value signals in eleven phases. Neither produced a written buyer description, a target list or a price test. The founder's answers add why that mattered more than it looked: Telkom came through Agate (Correction 2), so learning from it meant asking Agate how such leads arise, and no source records that question.
3. **The measures DEUS used were vanity metrics:** selection into programmes, a booth, MoMs signed (Phases 2, 5), team confidence (Phase 11), and on the public side "40+ brands" (deushcs.com), which the founder says counts brands inside about six paying groups (Fact sheet, Public claims). Actionable metrics (meeting-to-pilot rate by segment, repeat purchase, reasons lost in buyers' words, dev hours per client) are almost all NO DATA (Fact sheet, Funnel).
4. **The cheapest test was never run: using the products in-house.** DEUS rarely uses Discover or Enhance on its own candidates or team (Founder answers). Lost prospects name the game's length and small text (Fact sheet, Funnel); a team that took its own game for each hire would have seen that without a sale. Interpretation: this is a small point with a large signal, because it suggests the products are treated as things to sell, not things to learn from.
5. **Big batches, slow loops.** The Discover report took about a year (Phase 6); Enhance took three to four years and was rebuilt (Phase 10); the vendor switch cost almost a year (Phase 4). Each loop was measured in years, so DEUS learned a few things per product in the whole history. ASSUMPTION: with clear tests, the same period could have run dozens of small loops.
6. **"Build to feel ready" is the mental model behind it** (Step 1, Loop 5). Lean Startup names this exactly: building is used to reduce the founder's uncertainty, when only a buyer's behaviour can reduce the business's uncertainty.

**Said plainly:** the problem was never that the products were bad. Telkom's quotes, DKB's satisfaction and the Ko Will rebuild suggest they can be good (GBA deck, Success Story; Fact sheet; Recap, Phase 10). The problem is that DEUS spent its money and years answering "can we build it?" when the open question, every time, was "who will pay for it, and how do we reach more of them?". Sunk cost (the Rp1 billion, the four years of Enhance) is not evidence for either product, and neither is the dad's willingness to keep funding (Correction 5): it decides how long DEUS can look for the answer, not what the answer is.

## 5. The three cheapest experiments to run next

Each one is planned in reverse, as the method asks: what we need to learn, how we'll measure it, and the least we need to build (nothing new in all three). They replace the first pass's three: Experiment 1 now includes the Agate route, Experiment 2 now tests the DKB contract instead of the Enhance market (which has answered at Rp30 juta), and Experiment 3 now targets the loss reason the founder reports most.

### Experiment 1: Telkom-like assessment units, through Agate and introductions (Discover, growth and value)

- **Learn:** can DEUS reach organisations with Telkom's profile (a central assessment unit or an annual MT, graduate or campus programme with hundreds of candidates, open to gamified assessment), and will they pay? And does the Agate route, the only one that has produced such a buyer, send DEUS leads on known terms?
- **Measure:** (a) Agate: partner terms in writing (revenue share, price, exclusivity, how leads are shared among the five partners) and the number of qualified leads Agate passes DEUS in 60 days; (b) introductions: meetings with the head of an assessment unit or programme; (c) paid pilots from either route (minimum order Rp10 juta, Pitch deck A9).
- **Build:** nothing. A list of 20–30 named organisations with central assessment units or MT programmes; the Telkom slide and Ibu Lina's quote (GBA deck, Success Story); one meeting with Agate; introductions to listed organisations only, from the dad's network or Agate. Cold email to this list is not part of it: the founder reports that similar organisations have not responded to it (Founder answers, Step 3).
- **Time and cost:** 45–60 days of founder and Audrey time; no cash beyond the team.
- **Pass:** at least 1 paid pilot from either route within 60 days, **and** either written Agate terms with at least 1 lead passed, or at least 5 meetings with programme owners. **Fail:** 0 paid pilots, fewer than 2 meetings, and no written answer from Agate on how leads are shared. A pass means this is the beachhead and Agate is a channel to manage. A fail means the Telkom deal was Agate's sale more than DEUS's, and Discover needs the bigger pivot in section 4.

### Experiment 2: Write the DKB contract down and log its cost (Enhance, value capture)

- **Learn:** does the DKB contract earn more than it costs once its real cost is counted, and will DKB accept a written limit on what Rp35 juta includes?
- **Measure:** 30 days of hours logged by Ko Will and Thania on DKB work (support, fixes, new features, meetings), plus the Rp4 juta server (Fact sheet, Money); DKB's answer to a one-page "what the monthly fee includes" proposal.
- **Build:** a time-log spreadsheet; a one-page scope set by the founder from the first two weeks of the log, framed as a set amount of development time each month with extra requests queued for the next month (Step 1, Loop 4: DKB has refused a price per change, so a capacity limit is the softer ask; ASSUMPTION that DKB will accept it more easily).

## 6. The founder's question: "If I run Enhance as a DKB service contract, is that still fine as a business model?"

**Short answer: yes for the contract, no for the company.** As a way to run the DKB relationship, a service contract is the right call and better than what DEUS does now. As DEUS's business model it is not enough on its own: it covers only part of the monthly cost, it depends on one client and one engineer, and its scope is still unpriced. It is fine as a cash line that pays part of the bills while Discover is tested, if four conditions below are met.

### What "service contract" means here

In Lean Startup terms this is a **business-architecture pivot**: from a product sold to many buyers to custom work for one buyer at a high price. Many sound businesses are built this way (ASSUMPTION: software agencies and outsourced HR systems commonly run on retainers). What makes one sound is that each contract earns more than it costs, has a written scope, and that the firm can win more such contracts. Each test below is applied to DKB.

### The tests

| Test | DKB today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Does the client value it? | Rp35 juta a month, about 300–400 users, "quite satisfied"; pays above the Rp30 juta quoted to new clients (Fact sheet) | **Yes.** About Rp88,000–117,000 per user per month (derived from Rp35 juta ÷ 300–400 users) |
| Does it earn more than it costs? | Fee Rp35 juta; server about Rp4 juta, leaving about Rp31 juta (Fact sheet, Money; derived). Ko Will's and Thania's time on DKB: NO DATA | **Probably, not proven.** Illustration only (ASSUMPTION): if DKB took one person full-time at the team's average salary of Rp8–10 juta (Fact sheet, Money), the contract would leave about Rp21–23 juta a month for the rest of DEUS (derived: Rp35 juta − Rp4 juta server − Rp8–10 juta). Experiment 2 replaces this guess with a measured number |
| Is the scope written? | No. DKB adds features without paying extra and has never accepted a priced scope (Fact sheet, DKB row). Contract start, notice period and code ownership: NO DATA (Fact sheet, gap 4) | **No.** This is the main weakness: an unwritten scope grows with every free feature (Step 1, Loop 4) |
| Can it survive a departure? | If Ko Will left, keeping Enhance running "would be very hard" (Fact sheet, Team) | **No.** One engineer is a single point of failure for 100% of revenue |
| Does it pay for the company? | Rp35 juta against a burn of about Rp52–62 juta: about 56–67%, leaving a gap of about Rp17–27 juta a month, or about Rp21–31 juta if the server is on top (Fact sheet; derived) | **No.** It reduces the loss; it does not remove it |
| Can DEUS win more such contracts? | At Rp30 juta a month the dad's connections say no budget or "hold till later"; Apollo zero meetings (Founder answers; Fact sheet, Funnel) | **Not shown.** A service business grows by adding clients and people; no second client has signed since BCG left |

### What it would take to be fine

1. **Write down what Rp35 juta includes:** a set amount of development time each month, hosting and support; requests beyond it wait for the next month (Experiment 2). Also find and write the contract's notice period and who owns the code (NO DATA).
2. **Know its real cost:** log DKB hours for 30 days. If the contract does not clearly earn more than it costs, the free scope is the first thing to reopen, at renewal.
3. **Protect against the key-person risk:** Ko Will documents how Enhance runs (deploy, data, the DKB-specific parts) so a second person could keep it running. Step 1 ranks this as leverage point 6.
4. **No product spending on Enhance beyond what DKB pays for:** no new modules, website, or outreach for Enhance unless a second company signs a contract first. This is the line that stops a service contract from quietly becoming product development for a market that has said no (Step 1, Loop 4).

**What would change this answer.** If a second company signs a service contract with a written scope, Enhance becomes a small service line worth growing. If DKB leaves, or the time log shows the work costs as much as the fee, the contract stops paying for anything else. Either way, the question of how DEUS as a whole becomes profitable sits with Discover and the Step 8 verdict (rerun as task 5.2), not with Enhance.

## Key findings

1. **DEUS built a lot and measured almost nothing; the corrections don't change that.** Eleven builds or additions across eleven phases; at most two show a measured problem leading to a change, and no phase records a test with a pass line set in advance (Recap, Phases 1–11). The Phase 11 suite was a partnership, not a purchase (Correction 1): cheaper, but still an addition made to feel ready rather than a test.
2. **The two best signals came through other people's doors and were never turned into a next step.** DKB came through the dad; Telkom's Assessment Center came through Agate and paid twice (Rp380,000 per candidate in the second deal; the first is NO DATA) before its intake strategy changed (Recap, Phase 3; Corrections 2–3; Fact sheet). No buyer profile, target list or question to Agate followed. The channels DEUS ran itself have produced no deal (Fact sheet, Funnel).
3. **The loss reasons point away from more modules.** The founder reports "we already have our own assessment" as the most common reason, then the game's length and small text (Fact sheet, Funnel); nobody is reported asking for more modules. The only validated module is cognitive; the personality game DEUS owns has no validation, while the decks claim a validation and norming process (Fact sheet, Validation; Pitch deck, slide 8; GBA deck).
4. **Enhance as a DKB service contract is right for the contract, not enough for the company.** DKB pays Rp35 juta, is satisfied, and covers about 56–67% of the burn; the scope is unwritten, the cost is unmeasured, Ko Will is a single point of failure, and the market has said no at Rp30 juta (Fact sheet; Founder answers). Four conditions in section 6 make it a sound cash line.
5. **Three no-cost experiments in 60 days decide the next step:** Telkom-like assessment units through Agate and introductions; the DKB contract written down and its hours logged; a side-by-side pilot for prospects who already have an assessment. A written family funding amount would give the 60-day review a real deadline.
