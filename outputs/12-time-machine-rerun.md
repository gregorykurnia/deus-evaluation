# Step 12: The time-machine rerun

As of 2026-10-28. For the founder and the founder's dad; internal. Asked for by the founder: "if I were to do this business from scratch, what would I have done? Simulate one in which it works, as if there were a time machine."

**This is a simulation, not a forecast and not a finding.** Every line under **Then** is what happened, cited. Every line under **Rerun** is a judgement built from what Steps 1–11 found; where the rerun needs something the files can't confirm, it is marked ASSUMPTION. It uses only deal sizes DEUS actually achieved, so no revenue figure is invented; sums of those deal sizes are marked derived. **NO DATA** = number missing.

Sources: `inputs/DEUS_Company_History_Recap.pdf` ("Recap, Phase N"), `inputs/deus-fact-sheet.md` ("Fact sheet"), `inputs/Agate_Next_Chapter_GBA_deck.pdf` ("Agate deck"), `inputs/DEUS_Pitch.pdf` ("Pitch deck"), `outputs/01-systems-thinking.md` to `outputs/11-top-10-reasons.md` ("Step 1" to "Step 11").

---

## The answer

**With what DEUS knows now, a rerun would sell a service to one buyer before building anything: managed screening for large Indonesian groups with a yearly management-trainee (MT) intake, funded early by DKB-type system work sold as fixed-scope contracts.** In the simulation, that gets DEUS to revenue above today's burn with more than one paying client, using only deal sizes it really won. Even with the time machine, competitors, Agate selling the same games and the joint state-company recruitment could still sink it.

**The three changes that make the rest work** (each answers reasons 1–3 of Step 11):

1. **Choose the buyer before building:** group HR at large groups with a yearly hiring event. It is the only buyer type that has paid a large amount and come back (Step 3).
2. **Sell first, then build:** nothing is built until a buyer has paid for it or committed money to it (Step 1, leverage point 1).
3. **The first two hires are an experienced seller and a senior engineer;** the dad's role is written down, and family money comes in stages tied to milestones (Steps 6a, 8).

### Phase 1 in more detail: before building

- **Who to talk to:** HR and talent-acquisition heads at large groups. The questions: when do you hire at volume, what do you use now, what does it cost, what goes wrong. The aim is to find the groups already shopping for gamified assessment, which is what Telkom turned out to be (Recap, Phase 7).
- **The rule before spending:** three groups agree to pay for a pilot before the game is commissioned. This is a pass line chosen for the simulation, not data.
- **The build:** one short module that a candidate finishes in one sitting, with Sahid validating it from day one (the original had no validation; Recap, Phase 1). Today, the full suite is 3 hours 20 minutes of play (Pitch deck, slide 7).
- **The contract with Agate:** paid in stages tied to pilots; DEUS owns the game, or Agate may not sell it to DEUS's buyers. Today Agate sells the same suite directly from Rp49,999 per user (Agate deck). Whether Agate would have agreed is NO DATA.
- **The money:** most of the Rp1 billion stays in the bank until buyers have paid.

### Phases 3–4 in more detail: the fork when DKB walks in

- **Take DKB's money, not its roadmap.** Signed as a custom build with a written scope, a monthly fee and a price per change request (Step 1, leverage point 3). In the real history the feature list ran for three to four years (Recap, Phase 10).
- **BCG signing early is treated as a signal, not luck.** The rerun writes down the guess, "multi-brand groups with complex KPI setups pay Rp35–40 juta a month", and tests it: can DEUS find a third such group without the dad's network? If yes, Enhance becomes a product. If no, it stays a service line that funds Discover.
- **Quality from the start.** One senior engineer who sets the standard, the role Ko Will ended up playing in Phase 10 ("much better, even under a clear audit"). Client data comes in on a template, and cleaning is billed (Step 1, leverage point 6).
- **What that does to the money.** In the rerun BCG stays (ASSUMPTION; the recap records bugs and slow data cleaning around its churn, Recap, Phase 8). DKB plus BCG is Rp75 juta a month, more than today's whole burn of Rp52–62 juta (derived; Fact sheet).

### Phases 4–5 in more detail: who sells, and through whom

- **The first commercial hire has sold to HR at large groups before**, and starts with a 90-day target. Every hire gets a role card (Step 6a).
- **The dad's role is written down:** he opens doors only to groups with a hiring event or a KPI problem in hand, and he advises. He does not direct engineers; in the real history that created a second line of command (Step 6a).
- **The stop rule** (ten meetings in a segment with no deal) would have ended the retail wave in Phase 5 instead of running it on to Phase 11 (Recap, Phases 5, 9, 11).
- **No booths or awards as a sales channel** (zero conversions, Recap, Phase 5).

### Phases 6–7 in more detail: the Telkom moment

- **Not luck, the profile.** The real Telkom arrived already looking for gamification vendors (Recap, Phase 7). In the rerun DEUS has been looking for exactly that buyer since Phase 1, so one shows up earlier and more than once. This is the central bet of the simulation (ASSUMPTION).
- **After the first intake:** a debrief interview, a written case study, and a multi-year agreement discussed before the second intake ends.
- **Then the list:** every other group with its own MT intake, contacted on its intake calendar (Step 6b).
- **Product work follows what these buyers ask for.** The year spent digitising the report (Recap, Phase 6) happens only if buyers name the report as their reason.

### Phases 8–11 in more detail: what doesn't happen

- **No Potentia acquisition:** a different buyer; one deal of about Rp12 juta (Fact sheet).
- **No rebrand across ten industries:** the website names one use case and shows only paying clients' logos (Step 3, the "40+ brands" gap).
- **No new games bought up front.** MT screening normally tests cognitive ability, so buyers would probably ask for it (Step 7, C2). When they do, it is co-funded or licensed per candidate.
- **The team grows only when a contract pays for the seat**, which avoids most of the 15 departures out of 19 hires (Recap, Reference: people).
- **A weekly one-page sheet:** cash in the bank, the funnel per channel, and the reason for every "no" (Step 6a).

## Where the rerun ends up (Phase 11)

**One company, one buyer:** group HR at large Indonesian groups, with two lines.
- **MT screening run as a service:** seasonal, large deals. Telkom's size was Rp380 juta per intake (Fact sheet).
- **Custom performance systems** for two or more groups, as DKB and BCG were, at Rp35–40 juta a month (Fact sheet).

**The simulation's maths, using only real deal sizes** (all derived; Fact sheet):

| | Per year |
|---|---|
| Today's burn | Rp624–744 juta |
| DKB alone | Rp420 juta |
| DKB plus one Telkom-size intake | Rp800 juta: covers today's burn |
| DKB, BCG and two Telkom-size intakes | About Rp1.66 billion |

These are revenue figures, not profit: delivery cost is NO DATA, and Step 7 holds any "one deal closes the gap" claim until it is known (Step 7, C14).

**What "works" means here:** more than one paying client, revenue above burn, and a known reason why each buyer buys. Not a unicorn.

### The other timeline: Enhance first

The evidence cannot say which product to back: Enhance has about twice Discover's recorded revenue (about Rp900 juta against Rp440 juta plus unknowns), and Discover has the only repeat buyer (Step 7, MC3). A rerun built on Enhance would treat DKB and BCG as the first two of a product for multi-brand groups, keep BCG by fixing quality, and look for the third group without the network. It needs one thing the files can't show: that groups outside the family's circle have the same need (Apollo: zero meetings from hundreds of contacts, Recap, Phase 11). The main simulation keeps Enhance as a funded service and tests that question early.

## What could still have sunk it, even with the time machine

- **Competitors already serve this buyer.** Talentlytica and Talentics claim 300+ companies and name Telkom-group clients (Step 4, W3).
- **State groups are moving early screening into the joint recruitment:** 107 state companies and 1.4 million+ registrants in 2025 (Step 4, W7; Step 6b). The rerun would need private groups with their own MT intake too (Step 6b, S2).
- **The pool may be small.** Whether more than about 20 groups run their own MT intake is unknown (Step 6b, ASSUMPTION).
- **Why Telkom bought, and why it stopped, is unknown** (Step 6c). The simulation bets it was the need plus the service, not a one-off.
- **Agate might not have agreed** to terms that stop it selling the same games (NO DATA).
- **Hindsight.** The rerun already knows what Telkom looks like. In 2022 the founder would have had to find that out by asking, which is exactly the habit the rerun adds. Step 7 also notes the plainer reading of the history: an underfunded first-time team learning by doing (Step 7, C1).

## Key findings

1. **The rerun differs in three choices, not in luck:** a buyer chosen first, money before building, and a seller and a senior engineer as the first hires (Steps 1, 3, 6a).
2. **The pieces existed in the real history:** BCG and DKB paid before Enhance was finished, and Telkom came already looking. The rerun turns those signals into the plan instead of exceptions (Recap, Phases 3, 7; Step 2).
3. **On real deal sizes, the bar is low:** DKB plus one Telkom-size intake a year (Rp800 juta) is above today's burn of Rp624–744 juta, before delivery costs, which are unknown (derived; Step 7, C14).
4. **The time machine does not remove the outside risks:** incumbents serving the same buyer, Agate selling the same games, and state groups moving screening into the joint recruitment (Steps 4, 6b; Agate deck).
5. **Most of the rerun is still available today** and is already in the verdict: the buyer tests, the money bar, the funding limit, the DKB contract and the Agate terms (Step 8).
