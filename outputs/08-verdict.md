## The verdict in one paragraph

**PIVOT, with shrinking to what pays as the default.** DEUS as it runs today, three products sold to whoever is reachable, is not working: neither product has product-market fit (Step 5), no Discover or Enhance deal is recorded from any channel tried since Telkom (Recap, Phases 8–11; Fact sheet), all revenue is one client, and the company loses about Rp17–27 juta a month with an unknown amount in the bank (Fact sheet). Two assets are real: a client that pays Rp35 juta a month for Enhance, and one large buyer that bought Discover twice. So DEUS changes now: it stops everything that is not selling (Potentia, Apollo, untargeted outreach, new builds, hires, and probably the office), and it gives **two ways back to growth 60 days each**: Discover as a managed MT-screening service, and Enhance as a fixed-price system for groups like DKB. Each test must bring **money, or a commitment backed by money**, by **2026-12-27**. On **2026-12-28** the company keeps the product that passed; if neither passes, DEUS **shrinks to the DKB service** and the growth bet ends. If DKB gives notice before a test passes, DEUS **winds down in an orderly way**. The clock is set by a **written family funding limit**, not by hope: if it is not written by 2026-11-04, the decision moves to 2026-11-27.

DEUS has spent eleven phases building (a Rp1 billion game, a second product off one client, a digitised report, an acquired kids' product, a full rebuild, a new game suite, a rebrand) and almost none measuring (Step 2). Agate, which built the games DEUS sells, sells the same suite directly, with free pilots and an MT pitch of its own (Agate deck). The four people who stayed hold the seats that were clearly defined, but no one on the team has ever been hired to sell (Step 6a). **In short: the products are ready; the buyer, the channel and the cash plan are not.**

## 8. Recommendation: PIVOT, with shrinking to what pays as the default

**The call.** DEUS stops being a three-product company that sells to whoever is reachable. From 2026-10-29 it runs as a small company that pays most of its way (DKB) and gives two products **60 days each to bring money from one defined buyer type**. If neither does, it stays small. This is falsifiable: **DEUS grows again only IF test A or test B passes its line by 2026-12-27; otherwise it shrinks to the DKB service on 2026-12-28.**

### Stops now (no-regret, whatever the tests show)

- **Potentia:** zero team hours; the site's future decided by 2027-01-26.
- **Outreach:** Apollo; new F&B contacts (calls to existing F&B prospects for reasons only); waves of the dad's network to companies that fail the buyer filter (Step 3, section 5).
- **Building:** no new games, modules, Enhance features for anyone but a paying DKB change request, or rebrand work beyond two fixes: paying-buyer logos only, and validity claims backed or softened (Step 7, ACT list).
- **Hiring:** none until a test passes and the money is known (Step 6a, item 8).
- **The office (Rp12 juta a month):** give notice in week 1 at the earliest date the lease allows, unless the penalty is larger than the saving (lease terms NO DATA). A team that is not building and sells in buyers' offices does not need it (interpretation; ASSUMPTION that the team can work without it).

### What sets the clock

By **2026-11-04** the founder writes down the bank balance and every recurring cost (Apollo, Power BI, hosting, the Potentia site, Agate), and the founder and the dad agree **in writing** how much more family money will go into DEUS and until what date. The family's money buys time to test, never time to build.

- If the bank balance plus that written amount covers the gap to 2026-12-28 plus the cost of shrinking (severance and notice periods, NO DATA): the dates above stand.
- If it doesn't, or if any of these figures is still missing on 2026-11-04: **the decision moves to 2026-11-27.** A test that is on track on its day-30 line may then continue to 2026-12-27 only within the written amount; otherwise DEUS shrinks.

## Key findings

1. **The call is PIVOT, with shrinking to what pays as the default.** DEUS as it runs today is not working: fit D for both products, traction F, one client paying 100% of revenue, a monthly loss of about Rp17–27 juta or more, and an unknown bank balance (Step 5; Recap, Phase 11; Fact sheet). Two assets are real: DKB's Rp35 juta a month and the Telkom case.
2. **Which product is not settled by the evidence, so both get 60 days with one money bar.** Discover has the only repeat buyer; Enhance has about twice the recorded revenue (Step 7, MC3). Each must bring a payment or a money-backed commitment by 2026-12-27; anything short of that is a fail, and the winner is chosen on contracted value minus delivery cost, never on meetings.
3. **Stop now, whatever the tests show:** zero hours on Potentia; no Apollo, new F&B or untargeted network waves; no new builds or hires; notice on the office unless the lease makes it cost more than it saves; paying-buyer logos only.
4. **The clock is set by a written family funding limit**, not by the bank balance alone: if family money quietly covers the gap, no deadline is real (Step 7, section 4). Without it by 2026-11-04, the decision moves to 2026-11-27.
5. **The top three risks** are a clock that isn't real, all revenue on one client nobody has surveyed and one engineer, and DEUS being replaceable in its best segment (Agate, incumbents, the joint recruitment). The verdict is provisional until buyers have been asked why (Step 6c).
